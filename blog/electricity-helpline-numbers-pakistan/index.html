Quick answer

The three numbers covering most people

Most people need just one of three numbers so start here before going any further.

118 is the main helpline. It is confirmed on the official sites of IESCO, PESCO and K-Electric. It reaches a person rather than a menu.

8118 takes complaints by SMS. IESCO and PESCO both publish it on their homepages. That makes it the fastest written route to a record.

051-2013200 reaches NEPRA, the regulator sitting above every company. Use it once your own company stops responding to you.

Two big exceptions exist. MEPCO publishes a completely different number. HAZECO does too and neither of them publishes 118.

K-Electric consumers have their own shortcode as well. It is 8119, not 8118 and mixing the two wastes an entire afternoon.

Three national portals matter more than any phone number. Learn them and most problems shrink: complaints at ccms.pitc.com.pk, duplicate bills at bill.pitc.com.pk, new connections at enc.com.pk.

None of those portals need a long sign-up. An OTP on your registered mobile number is enough and the whole process takes under a minute.

Save your 14 digit reference number in your phone contacts today. Every portal and every agent asks for it first.

The rule

Why your own bill settles any argument

Here is the genuinely useful part. Your electricity bill must carry the helpline number and this is a legal duty rather than a courtesy.

NEPRA's Performance Standards for Distribution, notified as SRO 1430(I)/2026 on 25 August 2026, sets the obligation out in plain terms.

Every distribution company must earmark a consumer care or helpline number. Reason: consumers need a direct line to the company billing them.

Companies must publish this number on electricity bills and in their offices. They must publish it on their own websites too.

Change the number and they must tell consumers about it. The duty to inform sits in the same regulation.

So treat your latest bill as the final word on the digits. Use this page to understand what each channel actually does.

IESCO

Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Attock, Jhelum, Chakwal

IESCO serves the capital and the districts around it, covering roughly three million consumers across five districts.

Its homepage states the position in a single line: For Complaints and Customer Assistance, Call 118, SMS 8118.

That is the whole answer for IESCO consumers. One number reaches a person and one shortcode gives you a written record of the complaint.

Duplicate bills sit at bill.pitc.com.pk/iescobill so keep your 14 digit reference number handy before you open it.

Registered complaints go through ccms.pitc.com.pk, where no long registration is needed and an OTP on your phone is enough.

IESCO also runs a Consumer Profile portal at cp.pitc.com.pk. The portal shows twelve months of billing so nothing goes missing during a dispute.

Save 8118 as a contact today. A written complaint beats a phone call every single time money is involved.

PESCO

Peshawar and the rest of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa

PESCO covers most of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and its site carried both 118 and 8118 on the day of checking.

The company also runs a dedicated Complaint Cell page and a Customer Services section, both linked straight from the homepage.

Note one recent change. Hazara districts moved to a new company on 1 July 2025 and Abbottabad and Mansehra now belong to HAZECO.

Not sure about your company? Look at the top of your bill, where the company name is always printed.

PESCO publishes a load shedding schedule on its site as well. Bookmark it during summer months. Outages run longest.

Emergency shutdowns get their own page and safety notices appear there ahead of scheduled storm work.

A Know Your Customer drive has been running across the province, with unregistered connections being recorded door to door.

MEPCO

Multan, Dera Ghazi Khan, Sahiwal, Bahawalpur

MEPCO is the exception everyone gets wrong. It does not publish 118 anywhere on its official pages.

Its own footer gives a completely different number instead: 0319-9757789. The published email is rcc@mepco.com.pk.

We checked both the homepage and the Customer Care page. Both carry the same contact block, with no toll free line alongside it.

Do not trust any listing giving MEPCO an 0800 number. We found no such number on any official MEPCO page we could reach.

The company covers a huge footprint: Multan, Dera Ghazi Khan, Sahiwal, Bahawalpur, Vehari, Khanewal and more besides.

Complaints still run through ccms.pitc.com.pk and duplicate bills come from bill.pitc.com.pk/mepcobill.

MEPCO publishes its own Customer Service Manual online too, mirroring the national manual issued by NEPRA.

HAZECO

Abbottabad, Mansehra and six more Hazara districts

HAZECO is the newest company in the country. It split from PESCO on 1 July 2025 and took eight districts with it.

Its helpline is 0992-920211, a landline carrying the Abbottabad dialling code rather than a national shortcode.

The headquarters sits opposite Post Graduate College No. 1, Gulistan Colony, College Road, Abbottabad, should you need to visit in person.

Duplicate bills come from bill.pitc.com.pk/hazecobill. New connections and load extensions run through enc.com.pk, the national portal shared by all distribution companies.

Eight districts fall under it: Abbottabad, Mansehra, Haripur, Battagram, Kohistan, Torghar, Shangla and Upper Kohistan.

HAZECO is young. Its online services still mirror PESCO in places so expect some links to look familiar.

Check your company before dialling. A Karachi caller ringing a DISCO helpline wastes the trip.

The company also maintains a Facebook page and an X account, both named on its official contact page.

Social teams answer in public and public replies tend to arrive faster than private ones do.

NEPRA

When your company stops responding

NEPRA is the regulator. It does not fix your meter but it makes the company answer you.

The number is 051-2013200 and the same line serves both the head office and the Director General of Consumer Affairs.

Lines run 9:00 am to 5:00 pm, Monday to Friday. The email is cad@nepra.org.pk and the fax is 051-2600021.

Three conditions apply before NEPRA will move so know all of them before you dial.

  1. Complain to your own company first. NEPRA's form asks whether you did.
  2. Keep the complaint under one year old. Older cases are refused automatically.
  3. Sign an affidavit and note the form will not submit without it.
  4. Gather your documents: the disputed bills, the company reply and your CNIC copy.
  5. Keep files small. Each attachment must stay under 2.5 MB.
  6. Register online at the NEPRA complaint portal or use the Asaan Approach app.
  7. Note the complaint number, then track the status with it later.

NEPRA keeps ten complaint offices: Islamabad, Karachi, Lahore, Peshawar, Quetta, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Multan, Faisalabad and Gujranwala.

Pick the nearest one on the form and your complaint routes there automatically.

The regulator cannot replace your company. It can order a reply and companies do listen to those orders.

Files must be PDF or JPG and anything larger than 2.5 MB gets rejected by the uploader outright.

Prepare

What to have ready before you call

A helpline call goes badly without preparation. Five minutes of gathering saves twenty minutes of repeating yourself to a stranger.

  1. Find your 14 digit reference number. It sits near your name on every bill.
  2. Keep the latest bill in front of you. The agent will ask for the amount.
  3. Note the meter reading and take a photograph with today's date visible.
  4. Write the problem down as a single sentence, then lose your thread? Read it out.
  5. Keep a pen ready and ask for the complaint number before you hang up.

Call early in the morning where possible. Lines are quietest before ten and agents are freshest.

Avoid the last two days before the due date. Every agent is busy with billing disputes.

Ask for the agent's name too. It helps enormously on a second call.

Ask two questions every time. What is my complaint number and by what date will this be resolved?

Photograph the complaint slip. Paper fades and thermal ink disappears entirely within a year.

Store bills in one folder. A six month stack settles most disputes without any argument at all.

Check the billing period printed on each bill. A period longer than thirty days must be charged pro rata.

NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual spells this out clearly and excess units carry forward instead of inflating the slab.

Read the units against your meter on the same day and a photograph settles the question immediately.

Myths

Four numbers people quote and get wrong

Plenty of published lists are simply wrong and four mistakes cause the most trouble for ordinary consumers.

First, NEPRA's helpline is not 1915. That number belongs to India's National Consumer Helpline and Pakistani blogs copied it wholesale.

Second, 0800-66772 is often listed as a NEPRA line. We found it on no official page at all so do not rely on it.

Third, 118 is not universal. MEPCO and HAZECO publish their own numbers so check before you dial.

Fourth, LESCO has no confirmed 0800 number anywhere official and we could not reach its site to check further.

Spot a number on a blog with no official source behind it? Treat it as unconfirmed and ring it once before trusting it.

Official pages change without notice. A number correct in January may be dead by June.

That is the whole reason this page carries a checking date, printed at the top of the directory above.

Escalation

When the helpline goes nowhere

Sometimes nothing happens at all. The complaint number sits there and nobody calls you back.

Here is the ladder, from the cheapest step to the last resort.

  1. Wait out the published deadline and billing errors get seven days.
  2. Call again with the original complaint number, then ask for the status in writing.
  3. Visit the customer service centre and get a stamped copy of your complaint.
  4. Ask about a meter challenge. You pay a fee and the company tests the meter.
  5. Escalate to NEPRA with your documents and the regulator can compel a response.

Keep paying the part of the bill you accept. Note in writing you have done so.

Refusing to pay anything hands the company the advantage so do not give it away.

Write the words paid under protest on your complaint. Those three words carry real weight.

Keep every receipt. A payment record ends half of all billing arguments instantly.

Testing happens in front of you so ask to be present and watch the equipment they use.

The fee is small and a fast meter found beyond limits brings credit for two billing cycles.

Keep the test report. It becomes your evidence at the next stage of escalation.

Checklist

The full checklist in one place

  • Save your company's number in your phone before you actually need it.
  • Photograph your meter with the date clearly visible.
  • Find your 14 digit reference number on the bill, in the top section.
  • Write the problem down as a single clear sentence.
  • Call, then ask for the complaint number before you hang up.
  • Ask the resolution date and note it down in your register.
  • Follow up in writing once the published deadline passes.
  • Get a stamped copy of any complaint you file in person.
  • Escalate to NEPRA with bills, the company reply and your CNIC.
  • Keep paying the portion of the bill you do not dispute.

Print this checklist or keep it in your phone. It takes the guesswork out of every call you make.

Call 118. It is confirmed on the official sites of IESCO, PESCO and K-Electric. MEPCO publishes 0319-9757789 instead and HAZECO publishes 0992-920211. Your own bill carries the correct number for your area.
