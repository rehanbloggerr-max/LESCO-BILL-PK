Your bank or wallet says the payment went through. Your electricity bill still shows an amount due.

Electricity bill payment status not updated? The app and utility record may disagree.

That mismatch is stressful, especially near the due date. Start by checking the transaction in the same channel you used to pay.

The first payment may still be unresolved. Pause. No second payment yet. Save the receipt or transaction ID.

The payment app and the electricity company's billing record are separate records. One can show a payment before the other reflects it.

Quick answer

Payment unresolved? Start with the original payment channel. Check these five things:

  1. Open the bank, wallet, retailer, or payment app used for the transaction.
  2. Find the final status. Check whether the amount was debited.
  3. Save the receipt and transaction ID.
  4. Recheck the latest bill through the provider's official route.
  5. Contact the organization that can trace the missing record.

The first transaction needs a clear final status. Do not pay again before then. The official sources reviewed do not give one posting time for every provider and channel.

Electricity bill payment status not updated: why a paid bill looks unpaid

A bill payment passes through more than one record. The bank or wallet records the transaction. Three records. One payment. The payment network routes or settles the request. The electricity provider keeps the consumer's bill record.

These systems answer different questions. Your app may show receipt of the payment request. The utility's bill page can still show its earlier record.

A receipt helps trace the transaction. It does not always prove the utility has posted the amount to the correct account.

The opposite mismatch happens too. Your bank can show a debit without a final status. The payment status may still be pending. A debit alone does not confirm final settlement.

A duplicate bill shows the latest bill record from that service. It may not explain a bank transaction by itself.

Use both records. Check the payment history in the original app. Then open the latest bill through your electricity provider's official page or app.

No single wait time fits every payment

Online advice often gives a fixed wait such as one day or two days. The official sources reviewed for this guide do not publish one national posting deadline for every channel.

The payment provider can tell you whether the transaction is pending, settled, failed, or reversed. The utility can check whether a payment reached the consumer account.

A complaint deadline is not a payment-posting deadline. Keep those two timelines separate.

First identify the transaction status

Open the same bank app, wallet, retailer record, or payment channel you used. Find the transaction in its history.

Read the exact status shown there. Providers can use different labels for similar stages.

What you see What it may mean Safe next step
Successful or settled The provider reports a completed transaction Save the receipt and transaction ID. Ask the utility to trace the payment. Its bill may still show an unpaid balance.
Pending or processing The provider has not shown a final outcome Do not pay again. Ask the provider whether it is still processing or has failed.
Failed or reversed The provider reports an unsuccessful payment or returned amount Check your account balance and transaction history. Confirm the final status with the provider.
Money deducted, unclear status The debit appears, but the final outcome is uncertain Contact the provider with the transaction ID. Ask for a trace and written status.
Receipt available, bill still unpaid The payment channel has a record, but the utility page has not changed Keep the receipt. Ask the payment provider to confirm settlement, then contact the utility with its trace details.

Do not treat a notification preview as the full transaction record. Open the transaction details inside the app.

If the payment says successful

Save the receipt before contacting anyone. Check the consumer number, biller name, amount, and payment date on it.

A successful status is useful evidence. But don't discard the transaction ID.

Ask the bank or wallet to confirm the payment's final status. Ask for trace or settlement details. Then share them with the biller.

Then contact the electricity company or its official complaint channel. Give it the receipt and trace details.

If the payment is pending

Do not submit the same payment again. First ask the provider whether the request is still being processed.

A pending entry may later change status. Use the provider's own history to check for a final update.

Keep a screenshot of the pending status. Include the date and time in your notes.

A close due date needs quick contact with the utility. Ask about its process for tracing the pending payment.

If the payment failed or reversed

Check the transaction details and your account balance. Confirm whether the amount returned or whether the provider still shows a debit.

Do not assume a failed message means the money has already returned. Ask the provider to confirm the final financial status.

Only consider paying again after the provider confirms the first transaction failed or reversed. Check the bill details again before retrying.

If money left your account but there is no clear result

Treat this as an unresolved transaction. Do not share your app PIN, password, or one-time code with anyone.

Contact the bank, wallet, or payment outlet used for the transaction. Ask it to trace the transaction using your receipt or transaction ID.

Write down the complaint number. Keep the provider's reply with the bill copy.

How to check the bill without making another payment

Open the electricity company's official bill service. Use the reference number or customer ID printed on an earlier bill.

Confirm the company name first. Check the consumer name, billing month, reference number, and amount due.

Then compare the bill record with your payment receipt. Match the amount and the account number carefully.

A different bill total does not prove the payment posted. The new bill may include current charges, arrears, adjustments, or taxes.

Do not enter a bank login or wallet PIN on a bill-checking website. A bill checker does not need those details.

Try the provider's other official channel. The bill page may be unavailable during routine service interruptions. You can also contact its complaint center.

Who should you contact?

Start with the organization holding the record you need.

Problem First contact What to request
App shows pending Bank, wallet, or payment provider Ask whether the transaction remains pending or reached a final status.
Money deducted, no receipt Bank, wallet, or payment provider Request the transaction ID or trace reference.
App says failed, funds still missing Bank, wallet, or payment provider Ask for a trace and written confirmation of the debit status.
Provider confirms settlement, bill still unpaid Electricity company or official utility complaint channel Ask it to search for the payment using the transaction and consumer details.
Wrong biller or consumer appears Payment provider and electricity company Ask both organizations to trace the payment. Do not assume a reversal is automatic.
Payment made at a shop or counter The outlet or payment provider shown on the receipt Ask for the receipt details and payment trace. Keep the original slip.

A payment provider investigates the transaction. Two ledgers. One payment. Your utility keeps its own consumer-account ledger. One organization may need details from the other.

1LINK describes its Switch Dispute Resolution System as a service for member institutions. It includes bill payment disputes. It is not presented as a public consumer lookup tool. Ask your bank or wallet to raise or track the dispute through its own channel.

Contacting your bank or wallet

Use the support number or complaint option inside the official app. You can also use the contact details on the provider's official website.

Give the transaction ID, amount, date, biller, and reference number. Ask for a complaint number and the expected response process.

The State Bank of Pakistan requires banks to handle customer complaints under its consumer framework. The complaint process has separate timelines from utility bill updates.

The bank should explain its complaint timeline in the acknowledgement. Save that message.

SBP's 2025 framework sets separate handling targets for complaints filed with banks. Minor complaints have 3- or 7-working-day target paths, depending on category. Major cases have interim and final response rules. Fraud-related cases have their own deadline. These are complaint deadlines. They do not promise a utility bill update within the same period.

Contacting your electricity company or PITC

Use the official customer service channel shown on your bill or the provider's official website. PITC's Customer Complaint Management System lists calls at 118, SMS at 8118, and email at ccms@pitc.com.pk.

PITC's complaint system automatically acknowledges complaints within 24 hours after filing. It works toward resolving complaints within 14 days. It says complex cases may require an extension.

These are CCMS complaint-handling targets. Those complaint timelines do not promise a bill payment update within 24 hours or 14 days.

Ask the company to search for the payment against your connection. Share the reference number, bill month, payment amount, date, and provider trace.

Keep the complaint number. The first response may not resolve the issue. Reply through the same official channel. Request a status update.

What to keep before raising a complaint

Gather the records before you contact support. This makes the transaction easier to trace.

  • Electricity company and biller name.
  • Reference number or customer ID.
  • Bill month and payable amount.
  • Payment date and approximate time.
  • Bank, wallet, retailer, or other channel used.
  • Transaction ID, trace number, or receipt number.
  • App status and any confirmation message.
  • Complaint number and the provider's response.

Keep the original receipt. Save a copy of the current bill and a screenshot of the transaction details.

Share only the information needed to trace the payment. Hide unrelated account balances or personal data in screenshots.

Don't give a support caller your PIN, password, OTP, or remote-access code. Official support should not need those secrets to locate a bill payment.

Keep a short payment timeline

Write down each contact date and the response. Keep this log for calls with the bank or utility.

Date Contact Case number Status or response Next follow-up

Complaint date Utility or CCMS Complaint number Bill-ledger response Follow up through the same case

Keep the original receipt. Send copies only through official support channels.

A short message for support

State the provider, bill month, amount, payment date, and transaction ID. Add the electricity reference number. Add the complaint number too.

Ask two direct questions: Has the payment settled? Can you locate it against this consumer account?

Do not include your password, PIN, OTP, full card number, or unrelated bank details.

What if the due date is close?

Contact the payment provider and the electricity company promptly. Tell each organization the transaction remains unresolved.

Ask the provider whether the payment settled or reversed. Ask the utility whether it can locate the transaction against your account.

Do not assume a receipt automatically prevents late payment surcharge. Do not assume it stops disconnection or extends the due date.

The applicable bill terms and the provider's decision matter. Ask the utility for instructions during its payment review.

Before paying again, ask both parties how to prevent duplicate collection. Keep written guidance with your receipt.

A payment dispute can take time. Save every reply and follow up through the same complaint number.

Common mistakes to avoid

Paying twice while the first payment is unresolved

A second payment can create another transaction to trace. Check the first transaction's final status before trying again.

Trusting an SMS without opening the receipt

A text alert may not show the full transaction details. Open the original app and save its receipt or history entry.

Treating a duplicate bill as proof of transaction status

A duplicate bill shows utility billing information. Your bank or wallet records the payment transaction.

Use both records. Do not expect one screen to answer every question.

Contacting only the electricity company

The utility may need a transaction ID or provider trace. Ask the payment channel to confirm the transaction first.

Then share its response with the utility complaint team.

Sharing an OTP or account password

A caller may claim they can update your bill immediately. Do not share your OTP, PIN, password, or remote-access permission.

End the call. Contact the provider through its official app or published support details.

Believing an unofficial fixed timeline

Third-party pages may state a set number of hours. A universal posting deadline was not found in the official sources reviewed for this article.

Ask the original payment channel for its status. Keep its case number.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How long does an electricity bill payment take to show?

There is no single official posting time for every Pakistani bank, wallet, retailer, and electricity company. Ask your payment provider for the transaction's final status.

Should I pay again if money was deducted?

No. Get the first transaction's final status before paying again. Ask the provider to trace it.

The app says successful, but my bill still shows unpaid. What should I do?

Save the receipt and transaction ID. Ask the provider to confirm settlement, then send its trace details to your electricity company.

Where can I find my transaction ID?

Open the transaction history in the app or channel used for payment. Look for a receipt, reference, or transaction-details screen.

Does my receipt automatically prevent late charges?

A receipt does not automatically prevent late charges. Call or message the electricity company before the due date. Ask how it handles the unresolved payment.

Should I contact the bank or the electricity company?

Contact the bank or wallet about the transaction. The utility handles bill-ledger and account-posting checks for your connection. You may need to contact both.
