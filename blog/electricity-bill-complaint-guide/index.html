Your bill is wrong. You called the helpline twice. Nothing happened and the next bill arrived with the same mistake on it.

Here is the uncomfortable part. NEPRA counted 7,421,134 complaints recorded by distribution companies across financial year 2024 to 2025. That sounds like a country of people who complain constantly.

Now look at the spread across the country. One company, SEPCO, logged just 1,627 complaints for the whole year. HESCO recorded 165 complaints about voltage.

SEPCO serves 828,053 consumers. HESCO serves 1,261,005. NEPRA wrote plainly about those numbers.

The regulator said such low figures may reflect deficiencies in complaint registration mechanisms. It also called for a system covering complaints lodged through telephonic phone calls.

Read the line above again. The regulator doubts the phone complaints were ever written down at all.

This guide is about the gap between complaining and having a complaint. Everything below comes from NEPRA's own manuals and notifications.

Quick answer

The one thing that decides it

One thing decides whether your electricity bill complaint works. Did it get registered?

A phone call leaves you with nothing to point at. A registered complaint leaves you with a number, a date and a duty sitting on the company.

So do these four things in this order and stop worrying about the rest.

Write the complaint down. Two copies, one page, no anger in it.

File it at a place obliged to register it. The sub-divisional office, a Consumer Services Center, a One Window counter or the company portal.

Ask for the acknowledgement before you leave. It must carry the date promised for your reply.

Write both of those in a register at home. Follow up the day after the promised date slips.

Do only this and you are already ahead of most consumers. The rest of this page explains why it works and what to do next.

Why they die

Why an electricity bill complaint disappears

NEPRA's Performance Evaluation Report for 2024 to 2025 does not pull its punches. It names the problem directly.

Distribution companies recorded 7,421,134 complaints between them. K-Electric alone accounted for 23 per cent of that total.

NEPRA read this as a compliment. It described K-Electric's share as reflecting a more comprehensive framework for recording consumer feedback.

Then it turned to the quiet end of the table. SEPCO reported 1,627 complaints for the year. NEPRA offered two readings.

Either SEPCO runs an exceptionally efficient redressal system or the data is inaccurate. The regulator did not say either way.

On voltage complaints the gap is starker. K-Electric logged 167,850 of them. GEPCO logged 46,854. PESCO recorded 23,300 complaints about voltage. SEPCO logged 845 and HESCO logged 165.

NEPRA found the figure improbable. Two million consumers cannot all enjoy perfect voltage.

A complaint never entered in the register cannot be answered by anybody. Everything here serves one purpose. It gets your complaint into the register.

Your right

Registration is a duty, not a favour

Most people ask for a complaint to be filed. You do not need to ask. The duty already sits on the company.

NEPRA's Performance Standards for Distribution were notified as SRO 1430(I)/2026 on 25 August 2026. Regulation 5 covers complaint centres.

Every company must maintain complaint centres or customer service centres at each sub-division, division, circle and at company level. They stay accessible round the clock, all days of the week.

Regulation 5(5) requires an electronic database to record complaints received at those centres. A register is not optional equipment.

Now the clause worth memorising. Regulation 5(6) covers this. A complaint handed to any official of any sub-division goes straight to the complaint centre. There it is recorded, registered and redressed.

Hand your written complaint to any company official at any sub-division. That official now carries a duty to pass it on for registration.

Regulation 6 adds the Consumer Facilitation Application, the CFA. Each company must build it to record, register, process, measure and report complaints.

Schedule V lists what the CFA must let you do. Two entries matter most here.

Entry (l) requires auto escalation. A complaint missing its timeline goes up to higher management on its own.

Entry (m) requires you to register a complaint and view its status. Entry (k) lets you file a complaint about mismanagement or corruption by any officer or member of staff.

Proof

What you must walk out with

The piece of paper is the whole game. NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual tells you exactly what it should say.

The manual was revised in 2025. Chapter 10 handles consumer complaints.

Clause 10.1 says Consumer Services Centers shall be approached for all types of complaints lodged by consumers. One Window Operations sit inside company offices.

At those counters, consumers are given acknowledgement of the complaint with definite dates for their replies. Those are the manual's own words.

Clause 10.3.2 repeats it for billing. Every billing complaint shall be registered in the Sub Divisional Office, the Consumer Service Center or the One Window Operation.

At registration, dates for rectification shall be given to the complainant. All of these complaints are monitored by the company's own Complaint Cells.

So the acknowledgement needs three things on it. The complaint number. The date you filed. The date they promise to reply.

A slip with no date on it is a receipt, not an acknowledgement. Ask them to add the date before you leave the counter.

Keep the slip. Photograph it the same day. Paper fades and counters lose things.

Evidence

Four documents to gather first

Do not write a word until these four things are in your hand. A complaint without evidence is an opinion.

The disputed bill itself. The original paper, not a screenshot of the amount. The printed bill carries your reference number and the reading the company claims.

A photograph of your meter. Shoot the dial or display with today's newspaper or a phone screen showing the date beside it. A date you cannot prove is a date nobody accepts.

Your last three bills. Three months of consumption turns a sudden jump into an obvious error. One bill on its own proves very little.

Your payment receipt. Needed for the fastest category of all. Money you already paid reappearing as arrears gets fixed the same day.

Everything above fits in a single folder. Photograph the folder once and keep the pictures in one album on your phone.

Do this before the visit rather than during it. Staff take a request far more seriously once the file exists.

The document

How to write an electricity bill complaint in five parts

One page. Five parts. Nothing else belongs on it.

Part one, who you are. Your name, your 14 digit reference number, your CNIC number and a mobile number you answer.

Part two, what is wrong. One sentence naming the error. Compare the reading on the bill with the reading on your meter and state both numbers.

Part three is the timing. The bill month. The exact date you first noticed the difference. Any earlier complaint number belonging to the same problem.

Part four, the ask. Say what you want in plain words. Correction of the bill. Removal of the surcharge. Or testing of the whole meter at their cost.

Part five, the evidence. List your enclosures by name. Bill copy dated, meter photograph dated, three previous bills, payment receipt.

Keep the tone flat. Angry letters get read last. A short specific letter written by a calm person gets read first.

Print two copies. Hand over one, keep the second and make them stamp your copy. Then ask for the reply date in writing.

A filled example

A filled electricity bill complaint, line by line

Below is a complete complaint for the commonest case of all. The reading on the bill is far above the reading on the meter.

PartWhat it says
ToThe Sub-Divisional Officer, [your sub-division], [company name]
SubjectWrong meter reading on bill for [month], reference number [14 digits]
Who[Name], CNIC [number], mobile [number], connection at [address]
What is wrongThe bill for [month] shows a current reading of [units on bill]. My meter read [actual units] on [date]. The difference is [units].
WhenThe bill was received on [date]. I first noticed the difference on [date]. No earlier complaint was filed.
The askPlease correct the bill to the actual reading and issue a revised bill under clause 10.3.1 of the Consumer Service Manual.
EnclosuresBill copy for [month]; meter photograph dated [date]; bills for the three previous months
SignSignature, name, date, mobile number. Space left below for the complaint number and the reply date.

Naming the clause changes how the letter travels. Clause 10.3.1 gives seven days for exactly this error.

Leave a blank line at the bottom of your copy. Ask the counter to write the complaint number and the reply date into it before you go.

Where to file

Five places to file it, ranked by the record they leave

Every route below works. They differ in one respect: the strength of the record left behind.

  1. The sub-divisional office. Strongest route. It is named first in clause 10.3.2 of the manual. You leave with a numbered, dated acknowledgement in your hand.
  2. A Consumer Services Center. Equally valid. Clause 10.1 says these centres shall be approached for all types of complaints. They also operate round the clock.
  3. A One Window counter. Designed for this. The manual says consumers here receive acknowledgement with definite dates for replies.
  4. Your company's portal or CFA. Leaves a status you can check yourself. Schedule V of SRO 1430(I)/2026 requires auto escalation to higher management once the timeline lapses.
  5. The helpline. Weakest route. Take the complaint number the agent reads out. Write it down at once. Follow it up in writing within a day.

Calling is fine for a fault on the line. Calling is a poor choice for a disputed bill.

A disputed bill needs a paper trail you can show to the regulator months later. Use routes one to four for anything involving money.

The deadlines

Your electricity bill complaint: twelve official deadlines

Clause 10.3.1 of the Consumer Service Manual sets the clock for each kind of billing error. The clock starts on the day the complaint is received.

That is why the date on your acknowledgement matters so much. Without it, nobody can prove the clock even started.

Arithmetic errors get corrected forthwith by the revenue officer or the sub-divisional officer. Neither one should take a second visit.

Ask for the deadline in writing on your slip. Then the company has told you its own promise.

When nobody calls

When the deadline passes and nobody calls

The promised date arrives. Nothing happens. This is the moment most people give up. It is also the moment the rules hand you leverage.

  1. Work out the exact day. Count from the date on your acknowledgement, not from the date you remember filing. The two differ more often than you would think.
  2. Quote the clause back. Write one line naming clause 10.3.1 and the deadline for your category. Ask for the reason for the delay in writing.
  3. Go one step up inside the company. Schedule V of SRO 1430(I)/2026 requires auto escalation to higher management once a complaint misses its timeline.
  4. Name the officer. The same Schedule lets you file a mismanagement or corruption complaint against any officer or member of staff.
  5. Open a second record. Lodge the same complaint on the Pakistan Citizen Portal. You now hold two dated numbers instead of one.

Keep paying the part of the bill you do not dispute. Pay the other 5,000 first. Your complaint about the remaining 4,000 rupees then carries far more weight.

Stop paying altogether and the company starts a disconnection case. You will spend the next month defending the disconnection instead of chasing the error.

Second record

The Citizen Portal gives you a dated second copy

The Pakistan Citizen Portal is run by the Prime Minister's Performance Delivery Unit. It is a government channel rather than a power sector one.

Register at citizenportal.gov.pk with your CNIC and mobile number. The app also runs on Android, iOS and the Huawei AppGallery.

File against your distribution company. The portal issues its own tracking number and the company has to respond through the portal.

At the time of checking, the portal showed 4,757,556 registered citizens, 6,663,802 total complaints and 6,553,275 resolved complaints.

None of this replaces the company complaint. NEPRA's own form asks whether you complained to the company first.

Think of the portal as a second dated witness. One more number, one more date, one more place the delay is on record.

Step one up

Provincial Office of Inspection: the metering route

Sometimes the company simply refuses. The dispute is about the meter itself, about billing or about the tariff being charged.

Clause 15.1.2 of the Consumer Service Manual covers this. A consumer may file a complaint with the Provincial Office of Inspection. The POI handles metering, billing and the collection of tariff.

The power for this sits in section 38 of the NEPRA Act. The POI is the provincial body built for exactly these disputes.

Here is the deadline people miss. Anybody aggrieved by a POI decision may appeal to NEPRA within thirty days of receiving the order.

Thirty days from receipt of the order, not from the date on the order. Note the day the order reaches you.

Hold on to every document from the POI. The appeal runs on that file rather than on a fresh telling of the story.

Going to NEPRA

Taking an electricity bill complaint to NEPRA

NEPRA sits above every distribution company. Two separate routes lead there and they cover different ground.

Clause 15.1.3 covers the first route. Under section 39 of the Act, any consumer may file a written complaint with the Authority against a licensee. The complaint concerns contravention of the Act or of any order, rule, regulation, licence or instruction.

Those complaints are processed under the Complaint Handling and Dispute Resolution Procedure Rules, 2015.

Clause 15.1.4 covers the second route. Under section 35-A of the Act, a consumer may complain to NEPRA about six things. They are over billing, non-compliance with metering instructions, charges above the approved ones and disconnection for non-payment. Theft of electricity and use of energy for an unsupplied purpose complete the list.

Clause 15.1.5 closes the loop. The company shall comply with the orders, decisions and instructions the Authority passes on the complaint.

The online form sits at nepra.org.pk, under the Consumer Affairs section. NEPRA also runs a mobile application named NEPRA Asaan Approach for complaints of an urgent nature.

NEPRA warns on its own site to check the publisher before installing. Fake applications copy official names.

Mistakes

Seven ways a good case gets lost

Every one of these is common. Caught early, none of them is fatal.

  1. No acknowledgement was taken. The complaint cannot be proved to exist. Go back the same week and get the slip.
  2. The slip carries no reply date. The clock never started. Ask for the date to be added in writing.
  3. Everything was done by phone. NEPRA itself flags telephonic complaints as poorly recorded. Follow up in writing within a day.
  4. The whole bill went unpaid. A disconnection notice follows. Then pay the part you do not dispute at all. Keep the receipt inside the complaint file.
  5. The one year gate was missed. A NEPRA complaint older than one year cannot be processed under the 2015 Rules.
  6. The attachment is too large. Only PDF and JPG are allowed, at 2.5 MB per file. Compress before you upload.
  7. The wrong office got the letter. Clause 15.1.2 gives thirty days from receipt of a POI order. Note the day the order reaches you.

Six of these seven cost nothing to avoid. The seventh costs one calendar entry.

Write the reply date into your phone the moment you get the slip. That single habit prevents most of this list.

Checklist

The full checklist in one place

  • Photograph your meter with today's date visible beside the dial.
  • Collect the disputed bill plus the three bills before it.
  • Find your 14 digit reference number in the top section of the bill.
  • Write the complaint on one page, in five named parts.
  • Print two copies and keep a stamped one for yourself.
  • File at the sub-divisional office, the service centre or the portal.
  • Get the complaint number written on your copy before you leave.
  • Get the reply date written on your copy before you leave.
  • Photograph the acknowledgement the same day and file it.
  • Count the days from the clause 10.3.1 deadline for your category.
  • Pay the portion of the bill you do not dispute. Keep the receipt with the file.
  • Follow up in writing the day after the promised date slips.
  • Escalate to the Provincial Office of Inspection on metering disputes.
  • File with NEPRA inside one year, with PDF or JPG files under 2.5 MB.

Print this checklist or keep it in your phone. It takes the guesswork out of every call you make.

Sawalat

Aksar poochhe gaye sawal

How do I file an electricity bill complaint in Pakistan?

Write it on one page. File it at the sub-divisional office, a Consumer Services Center, a One Window counter or your company portal. Ask for an acknowledgement carrying the complaint number and the reply date.

How long does a billing complaint take to resolve?

Clause 10.3.1 of NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual sets the clock. Wrong meter readings get seven days. Detection bills get fifteen days to settle. A tariff change gets thirty days from the date of receipt. A paid bill shown again as arrears is corrected on the same day.

Can I complain to NEPRA without going to my company first?

You can file straight away. The form pushes back. Answer no to the question about the company. NEPRA then advises you to file with the concerned SDO or XEN first. A company complaint number makes the NEPRA filing far stronger.

Is there a time limit for a NEPRA complaint?

Yes. A complaint older than one year cannot be processed under the Complaint Handling and Dispute Resolution Procedure Rules, 2015. Older cases go in writing to the Office of DG Consumer Affairs NEPRA.

What files can I attach to the NEPRA complaint form?

PDF or JPG only, at a maximum of 2.5 MB per file. Compress photographs of your bill and meter before uploading. Oversized files are the commonest reason a filing stalls.

What if my company ignores the complaint after the deadline?

Quote clause 10.3.1 back in writing and ask for the reason for the delay. SRO 1430(I)/2026 requires the company portal to auto escalate a complaint to higher management once the timeline lapses.

Where is NEPRA's Consumer Affairs office?

NEPRA Tower, Attaturk Avenue East, G-5/1, Islamabad. Call 051-2013200 or e-mail cad@nepra.org.pk. Regional complaint offices also sit in Karachi, Lahore, Peshawar, Quetta, Hyderabad, Sukkur, Multan, Faisalabad and Gujranwala.
