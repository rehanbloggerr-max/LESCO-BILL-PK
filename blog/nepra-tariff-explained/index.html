Every rupee-per-unit on your electricity bill traces back to one regulator: NEPRA, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority. It approves the tariff that all eleven WAPDA distribution companies charge. It holds public hearings before any change. And it extends the quarterly adjustments to K-Electric consumers too.

Yet almost nobody can explain where the numbers come from. Why the rate jumped in July. What that FCA line means. Or why a relief suddenly appeared three months later. This explainer closes that gap. Who sets the tariff. How often it moves. And exactly how each movement lands on your bill.

If you only want the math, our electricity bill calculator applies the current schedule to your units in one tap.

Quick answer

Who decides electricity rates in Pakistan, and how often they change

NEPRA approves one uniform base tariff for the eleven ex-WAPDA DISCOs. That is LESCO, FESCO, GEPCO, IESCO, MEPCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO, TESCO and HAZECO. The base tariff is revised once a year, around July. On top of it, two mechanisms move your bill between revisions. The Fuel Charges Adjustment (FCA) applies monthly. The Quarterly Tariff Adjustment (QTA) applies every three months. Both can land as a charge or as relief.

That is why two neighbours with identical units can pay different amounts in different months. The base slab rates are the same. The adjustment lines differ.

The schedule

Current residential and commercial slab rates

These are the energy-charge rates set by S.R.O. 279(I)/2026, effective 12 February 2026. Our calculator applies the same ones. Here is the rule that trips most people up. Only protected residential consumers get the one-previous-slab benefit. Their first 100 units bill at Rs 10.54. Only the excess bills at Rs 13.01. Unprotected consumers get no such benefit. Every unit bills at the rate of the band your total consumption lands in. Lifeline consumers get no slab benefit at all.

Households that stay under 200 units for six consecutive months keep protected status. And by far the cheapest rates in the country.

What gets added on top of the energy charge

Your energy charge is only the starting point. Four more lines land before the total. First, the fixed charge. It bills per kW of sanctioned load, in the band your consumption falls into. For residential that is Rs 275 to Rs 675 per kW. Then the FPA, the fuel price adjustment, revised monthly. Then the QTA, revised every quarter. The current cycle runs Sep to Nov 2026. After that comes GST at 18% on the subtotal. Electricity duty adds 1.5%. And a flat Rs 35 PTV licence fee. A household that only watches its unit count will still be surprised by the fixed charge.

Always confirm against your own bill's tariff lines. NEPRA revises the schedule annually, and adjustments stack on top. The 12-company index links every official bill page.

The process

How NEPRA actually sets a tariff

The annual cycle is public and follows the same script every year:

  1. DISCOs file petitions. Each company submits its cost data. Power purchase prices, losses, operation costs. Then it asks for the revenue it says it needs.
  2. NEPRA holds public hearings. Consumers, analysts and companies can appear and object. The hearings are open, and the objections often trim the ask.
  3. The Authority issues its determination. NEPRA approves a consumer-end tariff with reasons on record, published on nepra.org.pk.
  4. The federal government notifies it. The notified tariff becomes the schedule every DISCO bills against. It is usually aligned to the July fiscal year.

In the 2026 cycle the Authority determined the calendar-2026 consumer-end tariff on 7 January. It recommended a uniform structure on 12 January. The government notified it on 13 January. One national schedule for all DISCOs.

Monthly line

FCA: the fuel line that moves every month

The Fuel Charges Adjustment reconciles what electricity actually cost to generate against what the tariff assumed. Fuel prices move monthly, so the FCA moves monthly too. It appears on the bill as a per-unit amount. Positive or negative. It multiplies across every unit you consumed.

On a 400-unit bill, a Rs 2 FCA line is Rs 800. That single line is why "same units, different total" happens more often than any slab change. Read the FCA line before blaming the meter.

Quarterly line

QTA: the quarterly adjustment, with 2026 examples

The Quarterly Tariff Adjustment reconciles the bigger cost items once a quarter. Capacity payments, T&D losses, use-of-system charges. NEPRA announces the number; recovery (or relief) lands on bills over the following three months. Two real 2026 determinations show both directions:

Q2 FY2025-26: a positive adjustment

NEPRA notified +Rs 0.3504 per unit. That recovered about Rs 8.674 billion across the bills of March to May 2026. It was uniform for all categories except lifeline, prepaid and incremental-package consumers. K-Electric got it too.

January–March 2026 quarter: a negative one

The Authority approved a negative adjustment of Rs 1.9857 per unit. That is roughly Rs 67.17 billion of relief. It passed through the bills of June, July and August 2026. K-Electric consumers were included again. The same three groups were excluded. Net-metering exports in that quarter were accounted at Rs 25.32 per unit.

The lesson: the QTA is not a fine or a favour. It is arithmetic, decided in public, three months after the fact.

The stack

What sits on top of the energy charge

The slab math is only the middle of the bill. The full stack, in the order bills usually print it:

  • Energy charges: units × slab rates
  • FCA / FPA line: the monthly fuel reconciliation
  • QTA line: the quarterly reconciliation, when applicable
  • Electricity duty and GST: the tax block, currently 18% GST
  • TV licence fee: a fixed Rs 35 on domestic bills
  • Late payment surcharge: only if the due date passed

A quick way to sanity-check any bill: energy + FCA + taxes should account for nearly everything. If a line cannot be named, ask the DISCO office for it in writing. Or start from our calculator and compare the totals.

Status

Protected vs unprotected: how the status is won and lost

Protected status belongs to the account, not the month. A household that consumes 200 units or less for six consecutive months gets the protected schedule. That can be as low as Rs 3.95 per lifeline unit. Cross 200 units in any month and the account falls to unprotected rates. That starts from the next assessment. There the first band alone costs Rs 22.44.

The status line is printed on the bill itself ("Protected" / "Unprotected" near the tariff code). If it changed without an obvious heavy month, check the six-month history on the online bill. One AC summer or one wedding month is usually the culprit. Status recovers the same way it was lost: six clean months. To check your own, see the protected or unprotected consumer guide.

Karachi

Where K-Electric differs

K-Electric files its own tariff petitions, so its base schedule has its own history and its own tariff names. But the adjustment mechanisms follow NEPRA's determinations. The same quarterly adjustments reached KE consumers for the same months. That included the Rs 1.9857 relief of mid-2026. The exemptions matched too: lifeline, prepaid and incremental-package billing.

Practically: a Karachi bill can differ from a Lahore bill at the base-rate level while moving in lockstep on the quarterly lines. The K-Electric bill pillar covers the KE bill itself.

Verify

How to verify any rate claim in two minutes

Rate rumours spread faster than notifications. Before trusting a forwarded screenshot:

  1. Open your latest bill. The tariff code and per-unit lines printed on it beat any WhatsApp forward.
  2. Check nepra.org.pk. Determinations and notifications are published there with dates and reference numbers.
  3. Run the calculator. Enter your units and category in our bill calculator. If the result is far from the paper bill, look at the adjustment lines. The difference is usually there, not in a rate change.
FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Who decides electricity rates in Pakistan?

NEPRA, the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority, approves one uniform tariff. It covers all eleven ex-WAPDA DISCOs after public hearings. The federal government then notifies it. K-Electric files its own petitions but follows the same quarterly adjustment determinations.

How often do electricity rates change?

The base tariff is revised once a year, around July. On top of it, the Fuel Charges Adjustment moves monthly. The Quarterly Tariff Adjustment moves every three months. Either direction.

What is the QTA on my bill?

The Quarterly Tariff Adjustment reconciles capacity payments, losses and system charges every quarter. In 2026 it added Rs 0.3504 per unit on March–May bills and cut Rs 1.9857 per unit on June–August bills.

Is the LESCO tariff different from FESCO or GEPCO?

No. The eleven DISCOs bill against one uniform NEPRA-approved schedule. Only three things make two bills look different: surcharges, losses and adjustment timing.

What is a protected consumer?

A household under 200 units for six consecutive months gets the cheapest schedule. Rs 10.54 for the first 100 units. Rs 13.01 for 101 to 200 units. Qualify for lifeline and it drops to Rs 3.95 and Rs 7.74. Crossing 200 units in any month drops the account to unprotected rates.

Does K-Electric follow the same tariff?

KE's base schedule comes from its own petitions. But NEPRA's quarterly adjustments apply to KE consumers on the same months. That includes the mid-2026 relief. Lifeline, prepaid and incremental-package consumers stay exempt.

Where can I see the official current rates?

On your own bill's tariff lines first, then in NEPRA's published determinations on nepra.org.pk. Our calculator applies the same current schedule if you want an instant estimate.
