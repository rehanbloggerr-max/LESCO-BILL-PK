Why do similar unit totals get different rates?

Your bill can show the same units as your neighbour's bill and still charge a different rate. The category matters.

This protected vs unprotected consumer Pakistan rate guide starts with the category printed on your bill. A protected consumer pays the lower residential rates. An unprotected consumer pays the rate listed for the consumption band reached. The difference can be large before fixed charges or taxes appear.

The protected test is specific. NEPRA's definition covers non-ToU residential consumers. Their usage must stay at or below 200 kWh each month. The test looks at the past six months. It is not a six-month average.

This guide compares the categories, explains the official rate table, and shows how to check your bill. It also explains the 200-unit test for long billing cycles.

Protected vs unprotected consumer Pakistan: how status works

NEPRA's residential tariff separates eligible non-ToU homes from other residential customers. The protected category uses a six-month consumption test.

A protected account must show consumption of 200 kWh or less in each month of the previous six months. A single low bill is not enough. The account's type and meter arrangement also matter.

An unprotected residential account does not meet that definition. It may have a month above 200 units in its recent history. It may also use a different metering arrangement.

The word "consistently" matters. The rule does not describe a six-month average. It describes a repeated monthly limit across the six-month look-back.

The rule is not a simple current-month check

Compare all six bills, not just the latest total. A home may use 180 units this month and still fail the test. One prior month above 200 can affect the six-month record.

A home using 200 units today does not become protected on that figure alone. The account must also satisfy the six-month test.

Do not assume a fixed penalty period from one high bill. The notified definition states the six-month test. Check the category printed by the electricity company after each billing cycle.

Time-of-Use accounts are different

The protected definition applies to non-ToU residential consumers. A Time-of-Use account follows peak and off-peak rates.

A low monthly total alone does not place a ToU account in the protected category. Check the tariff type on the bill before comparing rates.

Taxes, adjustments, and fixed charges sit outside these rates. Your bill may add other items. Check the final line before paying.

The published schedule includes a national rate structure for XWDISCOs and K-Electric. The applicable category and meter type still matter. Use your bill's own tariff line for a payment decision. See the full 2026 electricity slab table for the wider rate schedule.

Check what each rate column means

NEPRA schedules can show more than one variable-rate column. A uniform determined rate and a government-applicable rate may differ.

This table uses the consumer-end rates listed in the February 2026 schedule. Do not copy a number from another column without checking its label.

A schedule may also show an adjustment for a specific period. That adjustment is not the same as the base variable charge.

Before comparing websites, check the schedule date, consumer category, monthly band, and rate-column heading. These details explain many rate differences.

Bills can show an earlier schedule after a tariff revision. Use the schedule for the current bill month. Your current bill is the final check.

Do not mix tariff rates with the final bill amount

A rate of Rs 10.54 per kWh does not mean a 100-unit bill costs Rs 1,054 in total. That figure covers the variable energy charge only.

Fixed charges depend on the approved load. The tariff lists a monthly amount for each kilowatt of sanctioned load. Read our fixed charges guide for more detail. Taxes and adjustments can change the final total.

Use the rate table to compare categories. Use the bill itself to check what the company charged.

How fixed charges work

The schedule lists fixed charges in rupees per kilowatt per month. The relevant kilowatt figure is the sanctioned load printed on the bill.

For example, the protected 101-to-200 band lists Rs 300 per kW each month. A two-kilowatt sanctioned load gives a Rs 600 fixed charge for that band.

That fixed amount is separate from the variable charge for each unit. Do not multiply the fixed charge by the units consumed.

The company may show fixed charges on a different bill line. Read the tariff label and unit carefully. A figure marked Rs/kW/month is not a per-unit rate.

Adjustments and taxes come later

Fuel and quarterly adjustments can change the amount billed for a period. Their values can change over time.

Taxes and duties also depend on the bill and current rules. This guide does not add them to the examples above.

A correct estimate needs the tariff category, monthly units, sanctioned load, bill period, and current adjustment lines. It also needs any tax or arrears shown on the invoice.

Use the calculator only as an estimate. Compare its assumptions with the latest bill before relying on the result.

How the slab calculation works

Protected and unprotected accounts do not use the same slab method. NEPRA's schedule gives protected residential consumers the benefit of one previous slab.

For a protected account using 150 units, the first 100 units use the Rs 10.54 rate. The remaining 50 use Rs 13.01.

An unprotected account uses the variable rate listed for its monthly band. At 150 units, the applicable 101-to-200 band rate is Rs 28.91 per unit.

This difference can make two bills look far apart. But the meter may record identical usage. The category and rate method still change the energy charge.

What the rate difference means in rupees

These examples use the notified variable rates. They exclude fixed charges, adjustments, taxes, arrears, and other bill lines.

Example Calculation Variable energy charge
100 units, protected 100 × Rs 10.54 Rs 1,054
100 units, unprotected 100 × Rs 22.44 Rs 2,244
150 units, protected 100 × Rs 10.54 + 50 × Rs 13.01 Rs 1,704.50
150 units, unprotected 150 × Rs 28.91 Rs 4,336.50
200 units, protected 100 × Rs 10.54 + 100 × Rs 13.01 Rs 2,355
200 units, unprotected 200 × Rs 28.91 Rs 5,782
201 units, unprotected 201 × Rs 33.10 Rs 6,653.10

The category gap is plain at 200 units. A protected account has Rs 2,355 in variable energy charges. The unprotected example reaches Rs 5,782.

At 201 units, the unprotected band rate changes to Rs 33.10. The energy charge becomes Rs 6,653.10 before other bill lines.

These calculations are not estimates of a complete bill. They do not include the fixed charge for sanctioned load. They also exclude monthly adjustments and taxes.

Use the electricity bill calculator for a broader estimate. Confirm its category and sanctioned-load inputs first.

What happens at 201 units?

The 201-unit example uses the 201-to-300 unprotected rate for all 201 units. It does not charge only the extra unit at Rs 33.10.

A small rise in usage can create a large energy-charge change. The applicable category and slab method both matter.

A reading period longer than a calendar month needs a separate check. NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual describes a prorating rule for longer billing cycles.

How to find your category on the bill

Start with the current bill, not an old screenshot. Find the tariff or consumer category area near the account details. Our bill status guide explains how to open the latest invoice.

Look for a residential tariff code and any printed protected or unprotected status. Some bill layouts show the category clearly. Others show a rate or tariff table instead.

Use the official schedule as a rate check. Next, list each of the six monthly unit totals in date order. Keep the bill dates beside them.

A current total below 200 does not prove protected status. The previous months form part of the test.

Confirm the field label before using its number. Customer ID, meter number, and reference number do not show the protected category.

Check the six monthly readings

Write the units from each of the last six bills in date order. Do not use the rupee total for this check.

A simple record can prevent a mistaken comparison. Note the bill month, units, reading dates, and printed tariff category.

Keep the bill period beside the unit total. A 33-day reading does not match a 30-day month directly.

Mark whether the meter is Time-of-Use or non-ToU. The protected definition applies to non-ToU residential consumers.

A bill with an unusually long reading period needs extra care. The number of billed days can affect the recorded units for that cycle.

Ask your electricity company to explain an unclear tariff line. Keep its reply and any complaint number.

Mistakes that distort the comparison

Comparing the bill total with a per-unit rate

The total includes more than energy charges. Fixed charges, adjustments, taxes, and arrears may also appear.

Compare the tariff rate with the variable-charge line. Do not compare it with the final payable amount.

Treating protected status as a monthly average

A low month does not erase the earlier record. NEPRA's definition uses each month in the six-month look-back.

Write the six monthly unit figures down. That makes an unusual month easier to spot.

Assuming every meter follows this table

The main comparison covers non-ToU residential service. Time-of-Use meters have peak and off-peak rates.

A business or agricultural connection also uses a different schedule. Check the tariff code before applying residential examples.

Reusing an old rate table

Tariff schedules can change. An old bill may show rates from an earlier schedule.

Use the current tariff schedule and the rate printed on your current bill. Check the month before comparing figures from another website.

Calling a proposal a new rule

Committee discussions do not change a bill by themselves. A tariff change needs formal approval and notification.

Until then, compare your bill with the notified schedule. Treat news about a possible change as a proposal.

A long billing period can change the 200-unit check

A reading taken after more than 30 days can show more than 200 units. That does not always mean the customer used more than 200 units in a normal month.

NEPRA's revised Consumer Service Manual gives an example. A reading of 208 units over 33 days is prorated to 189 units for a 30-day month.

Under the manual's example, the 189 units are charged under protected status for that month. The remaining 19 units carry forward to the next billing cycle. Protected status remains intact for the current month.

Check the number of days shown on the bill. Do not compare a 33-day reading directly with a 30-day threshold.

For a long billing cycle, ask the company to explain its prorating. Save the bill and reading dates.

Lifeline, protected, and unprotected are different

Lifeline is a separate low-usage category in the residential tariff. It has its own rate bands.

The 2026 schedule lists Rs 3.95 for up to 50 units. It lists Rs 7.74 for 51 to 100 units. These rates should not be confused with protected rates.

Protected rates apply to qualifying non-ToU residential consumers. The schedule lists Rs 10.54 for 1 to 100 units and Rs 13.01 for 101 to 200 units.

An unprotected account uses the rate for its applicable monthly band. It can include consumers who do not meet the protected definition.

Your connection type and sanctioned load determine the applicable tariff. Check the bill category before using any example.

K-Electric and other distribution companies

NEPRA's February 2026 decision covers XWDISCOs and K-Electric. It sets out consumer-end schedules and tariff changes.

A customer should still use the company name and tariff category printed on the bill. A ToU account follows peak and off-peak charges. It should not be compared with the non-ToU table above.

For a K-Electric bill, check the current K-Electric bill and tariff information. For a DISCO bill, use that company's official portal or customer support channel.

The category comparison in this guide does not replace a company review of an individual account. Ask the company to explain an inconsistent category.

What to do if the bill says unprotected

First, check the tariff code and meter type. Confirm the account is residential and non-ToU.

Next, gather the last six bills. Note each month, its units, and the number of reading days.

Look for a month above 200 units. Also check for a long billing cycle or a corrected meter reading.

Ask the electricity company to review any unexplained category through an official channel. Request a list of the six billing months it used.

Include the bill reference in your complaint. Keep a copy of the bill and the company's response.

A provider or payment app handles its transaction record. The electricity company handles the tariff category on its bill.

Do not send an account password, PIN, or one-time code. Use the official contact details printed on the bill or published on the company's website.

Proposed changes do not automatically change your bill

A parliamentary committee endorsed a proposal to remove the six-month limit. That endorsement does not itself change the notified tariff.

The National Assembly press release records the committee's endorsement on 16 September 2026. It is not a tariff notification.

The IMF's 2026 report describes a planned shift from the existing electricity subsidy structure to a targeted framework. It gives an end-January 2027 milestone for that reform.

That subsidy reform is separate from a formal change to the protected consumer definition. A planned subsidy reform does not prove today's category test has changed.

Check NEPRA and Power Division notices for formal changes. Until a new schedule is notified, use the tariff shown on your current bill.

Frequently asked questions

Is protected status based on a six-month average?

No. NEPRA's definition says 200 kWh or less per month consistently for the past six months. It does not describe an average.

Does using 200 units this month make me protected?

Not by itself. The account must meet the monthly limit across the six-month look-back. Check the status printed on your bill.

Is lifeline the same as protected?

No. Lifeline has separate low-usage bands. Protected rates apply to qualifying non-ToU residential consumers.

Are the listed rates my full bill amount?

No. They are variable tariff charges. Fixed charges, monthly adjustments, taxes, and other bill items can change the total.

What if my bill covers more than 30 days?

NEPRA's manual describes prorating for a longer billing cycle. Check the billed days and ask your company how it calculated the units.

Can a Time-of-Use consumer qualify as protected?

The notified protected definition refers to non-ToU residential consumers. ToU accounts follow peak and off-peak rates.

Where can I check the tariff on my bill?

Look near the residential tariff code and consumer details. Ask your electricity company to confirm any unclear category.
