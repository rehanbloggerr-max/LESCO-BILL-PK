Find the schedule for your connection

Wondering when the power will be off? Start with your electricity company and the feeder serving your connection. There is no single load shedding schedule for every household in Pakistan, and even nearby streets can be on different feeders.

Keep a recent bill handy. Find your provider and reference or account number, then check its official schedule. If you do not know your feeder, ask the company to identify it. Before making plans, check the dates and time windows on the notice.

Quick answer

Quick answer

Use your provider's official load-shedding schedule or customer service to find your feeder timing. Also check maintenance notices if relevant. A published schedule can change and will not necessarily explain an unexpected fault or guarantee a restoration time.

Guide section

How to check your feeder's load shedding schedule

  1. Identify your electricity company. Look at a recent bill for the name of your provider, such as LESCO, IESCO or K-Electric. Use the company that serves your connection, not simply the one serving a nearby neighbourhood.
  2. Keep your reference or account number ready. The exact identifier requested depends on the provider. If you need help finding it, see our guide to the reference number on an electricity bill or learn the difference between a reference number and customer ID.
  3. Find your feeder name or code. Check your bill or your provider's official resources if they show it. Not every bill or portal displays a usable feeder name. If you cannot find yours, ask your electricity company's customer service or local office to confirm it.
  4. Open the relevant official schedule. Choose the appropriate load-shedding or load-management page. If schedules are organised by circle or office, select the relevant area and open a suitably dated document, if available.
  5. Match the details. Check the feeder name, applicable dates, time windows and any notes. If the information is missing, outdated or unclear, ask the provider to confirm the schedule rather than guessing.

Keep your account details private. Enter them only on trusted official channels, and do not post a complete bill or account number publicly when asking for help.

The format varies by provider. Some pages offer an account lookup; others link to documents for different areas. If an official page is slow or unavailable, use the provider's customer service or local office for help.

How to read a feeder schedule

First make sure the schedule belongs to the company supplying your connection. Then check these details together:

  • Feeder or area: Match the exact feeder name or code where the document uses one. A similar street or neighbourhood name alone may not be enough.
  • Circle or grid: Some providers organise notices by circle, office or grid. Use the grouping given by your company.
  • Effective period: Look for the publication date and the days or dates covered. Do not assume an older or future-dated notice applies today unless its stated period includes today.
  • Time window: Read the listed start and end times carefully, including any date change around midnight.
  • Notes or revisions: Check for qualifications, updates and later notices. Confirm with the provider if two notices conflict.

The feeder serving a connection can change. If the name on an old bill or document no longer matches what the company tells you, ask for the current feeder details and use the latest applicable notice.

Guide section

Load shedding, maintenance or an unexpected fault?

Not every power cut is scheduled load shedding. Looking in the right place can save time, but a schedule alone cannot diagnose an outage.

Type of interruption What it means for your check
Load shedding A company may publish load-management timings for affected feeders. Check the provider's applicable schedule.
Planned maintenance or shutdown Network work may be listed in a separate maintenance or shutdown notice, with its own dates and affected areas.
Unexpected fault or other interruption Weather, equipment problems or another unplanned event may interrupt supply outside a published schedule. Ask the provider for the cause and updates.

If your power goes off outside a listed load-shedding window, it could be maintenance, a fault or another interruption. Ask your company for an update rather than assuming the cause.

Guide section

What if the load shedding schedule is not available?

Start by checking the provider name and the reference or account number on your bill. If you have a feeder name, check its spelling and confirm that the notice covers your area and date. For IESCO or another provider using downloadable files, make sure you opened the relevant circle and period.

Can't open the page or find a matching entry? Contact your company's customer service or local office. Ask which feeder serves your connection and where to find its latest applicable schedule. If you cannot get online, use the official contact details on your bill or visit the relevant office.

No result does not automatically mean your area is exempt from load shedding. The lookup might be unavailable, the details might not match, or the information may be published elsewhere. Ask your provider to clarify.

Guide section

What to do if the power is out outside the schedule

If the power goes off unexpectedly or stays off beyond the listed window, check your provider's maintenance notices and official updates. Then report the interruption through its official complaint channel. Have your location and account or reference number ready, and note when the power went off. Ask for a complaint number and an update, and keep the acknowledgement for follow-up.

If the matter remains unresolved, follow up with the company using the same complaint number. You can also consult the current instructions on NEPRA's consumer affairs complaint portal after approaching your provider. The registration page advises contacting the relevant SDO or XEN first and gives separate instructions for complaints more than one year old. A complaint does not itself guarantee an immediate response or restoration time. Our electricity bill complaint guide explains how to keep a useful record when contacting the company.

Safety first: Stay away from fallen wires and damaged electrical equipment. Do not touch them, open a meter or attempt repairs yourself. Report the hazard through official utility or emergency channels.

Frequently asked questions

Frequently asked questions

How can I find my feeder name if it is not printed on my bill?

Ask your electricity company's customer service or local office. Give your account or reference number through an official channel and ask which feeder currently supplies your connection. Some providers also show feeder information in their official apps or schedule tools.

Can I check a schedule using my reference or account number?

Some providers offer that option. The LESCO schedule page is indexed as requesting a reference number, while K-Electric's official page offers account-number searches. Availability and results can vary; contact your provider if the page does not work or the result is unclear.

Are maintenance shutdowns and load shedding the same?

No. Load management and planned network maintenance are different reasons for interrupting supply. Providers may publish separate schedules or notices for them, so check both where relevant.

Why does a nearby street have a different outage?

Nearby connections may be supplied by different feeders or affected by different parts of the network. A local fault or maintenance work can also affect one area without affecting another. Ask your provider which feeder serves your connection.

Does an empty result mean my area is exempt?

Not necessarily. A missing or empty result may have several explanations, including an unavailable lookup or a schedule for a different period. Confirm with your electricity company before assuming there is no scheduled outage.

Will the electricity return exactly when the schedule says?

A listed window is useful for planning, but it is not a guarantee. Timings can be revised, and unrelated faults or other interruptions can occur. Contact the provider for updates if supply remains off.

Final step

Keep your bill and feeder details handy, then check your own provider's official notice for the relevant date. If the schedule or your feeder is unclear, ask the company before relying on a timetable shared by someone else.
