Your IESCO bill never arrived. Maybe it slipped away before you could pay it. Your billing record already sits online, so you can open it yourself in under a minute.

An IESCO duplicate bill is the official re-print of your current month's bill. Same consumer name, same address, same payable amount and the same due date. It carries full validity for payment.

This guide walks the whole task in plain steps, one screen at a time. For the wider bill picture, our IESCO bill guide walks through every charge and payment route. The fixes get full sections too.

In a hurry? Open our IESCO checker and let it take you to the official page in one tap. The current bill opens in a new tab.

Quick answer

How to get your IESCO duplicate bill online

You can get your IESCO duplicate bill free from the official PITC page. Enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 10-digit customer ID works too. Then download the PDF or print the copy, and keep it for later. Banks and wallets accept this copy for payment, just like the original.

The official IESCO home page labels this service Get Duplicate Bill. The button sits right there on the home page. It leads into the PITC bill system.

No account is needed. No fee applies. The lookup runs on any phone browser at any hour.

Next, read the six steps below. They give the full route, with a check at every stage.

Basics

What counts as an IESCO duplicate bill

The name sounds formal. The document is fully official. Here is what the copy contains and what it is for.

It is an official re-print, not a new bill

The duplicate comes from the same billing record behind your paper bill, and nothing gets edited on the way. The figures arrive exactly as the system holds them, so the copy is as valid as the original. Then keep it as your working copy and pay from it.

The amount and due date stay the same

A duplicate shows the current month's payable amount and its due date. Nothing extra is added for using the online copy.

But the amount can change once the due date passes, because a late payment surcharge then appears. That fee is avoidable.

Banks, ATMs and post offices accept it

A printed duplicate goes through the same bank counters as any normal paper bill. Banks, ATMs and post offices all accept it. A clerk reads the reference number and the amount, while wallet apps need only the reference number. Pay from the app without printing anything at all.

Four moments when people need one

The bill never reached the house at all. It arrived torn or faded. Someone is handing the property over to a buyer. A bank or employer asked for proof.

For a property handover, pull the current copy on the spot and match the meter reading date first.

When the sub-division office still matters

A few tasks need a stamped or certified copy. Legal paperwork and some policy matters fall in this group, and the plain PDF may not pass there.

Visit your sub-division office with the CNIC and reference number. Staff will tell you the fee.

Step by step

Check your IESCO duplicate bill in six steps

The full lookup takes one phone and one number. Most readers finish inside a minute. The official PITC page loads your bill straight from the live IESCO billing system.

  1. Open the IESCO checker on our site.
  2. Read the 14-digit reference number from any old bill.
  3. Type the digits in the reference field and press search.
  4. Check the month, amount and due date on screen.
  5. Download the PDF or print the copy.
  6. Pay through your bank app, wallet or a bank branch.

Enter digits only. Then leave out the U or R letter from some IESCO bills. Leave out spaces and hyphens too.

Some bills label the number as a consumer number. Count the digits before you submit anything.

The reference field and the customer ID field are separate. A 10-digit customer ID fails in the reference box. See our reference number vs customer ID guide for the full match.

Find the number

Where to find your reference number again

The number hides in plain sight on every bill. One of these routes will bring it back.

Look at the top-left of an old bill

The reference number sits in the top-left block of every IESCO bill. It appears just under the consumer name and address.

Our reference number location guide labels the exact box for you.

Read the print carefully

The number runs 14 digits and gets printed in English and Urdu. Some bills add a U or R letter beside it.

Copy all 14 digits in one go. Start with a clear photo, then type. Careful beats fast here.

Try the customer ID instead

The 10-digit customer ID sits in the same block, near the reference number. Most IESCO lookups accept it as a full alternative.

This route saves time on a faded or torn bill. A shorter number also means fewer typing slips.

Mine your old receipts

Wallet apps and bank apps store an electricity payment history going back months. The receipt usually shows the reference number or the customer ID.

So screenshot the receipt before it scrolls away. That copy stays readable forever.

Ask the last payer

In a rented house or shop, the previous tenant usually paid the bill. A photo of any recent copy solves the problem.

The owner or a family member can send a quick picture too. Verify the address before you use the number.

Fix it

IESCO duplicate bill not showing? Fix these first

A blank result has a short list of causes. Work through them in this order.

The new month is not uploaded yet

IESCO posts bills in batches after meter reading rounds. Your sub-division may sit in a later batch. The page can still show the previous month for a day or two.

Then recheck the next day and read the issue date on screen. Never pay an old month twice. One month, one payment.

A valid number returns an error

Compare the number against the photo one more time. Then watch the middle digits most. Slips cluster there.

Also confirm the field. The reference box rejects a 10-digit customer ID.

The page is stuck or slow

The official page gets busy around due dates. A frozen form often clears after a short wait. A stable connection helps too.

Refresh once, then wait a minute. Repeated taps can send the same request many times.

The screen shows an older bill

This usually means the batch update has not reached your area yet. Nothing is wrong with your phone or connection.

Compare the bill month with today's date before you pay. An old copy does not demand anything. Check the month every time.

The meter or connection changed

A new meter or a shifted connection can bring a fresh reference number. The old digits may stop opening the current bill.

Use the number printed on the latest bill. For a stubborn failure on the old one, call 118.

Call the 118 helpline

IESCO answers billing questions on 118. Keep the reference number and CNIC nearby before the call. Note the complaint number afterwards.

The IESCO website lists 118 for calls and SMS 8118 for messages. For wider portal failures, our bill not found guide covers more cases step by step.

Next month

Stop needing a duplicate every month

A few small setups end this problem for good. Choose what suits your routine.

  1. Register the mobile number for SMS bill alerts on the official route.
  2. Keep a folder for every month's PDF.
  3. Snap one full photo of each paper bill.
  4. Set a reminder two days before the due date.
  5. Add the IESCO biller inside your wallet app.
  6. Store the reference number in your phone notes.
  7. Check the online bill at the start of each month.
  8. Print one spare copy for bank visits.

The SMS route brings the amount and due date straight to your phone. No more guessing from the doorstep.

One folder per year keeps every month lined up. Name the folder by year and skip the paper pile. So set it up once.

Print and save

Print or save the copy for later

The bill opens like any web page. Your browser then turns it into a file or a sheet of paper.

  • Use the browser menu and choose Print.
  • Pick Save as PDF on Android or iPhone.
  • Check the month and amount on the preview screen.
  • Name the file with the month, like iesco-2026-10.
  • Keep another copy in cloud storage or email.
  • Print one extra copy for bank visits.

On a phone, the share sheet hides the PDF option behind one tap. Open the page menu first. The choice appears as Print or Save as PDF.

Save the PDF before you print anything. Then print the full page, barcode strip included. A cropped print can get refused at a bank counter.

So check three values before saving. The month, the payable amount and the due date.

Pay

Pay with the downloaded copy

Paying from the copy works through every standard channel. Also, the reference number ties the money to your account.

  1. Enter the reference number in your bank app or wallet.
  2. Or carry the printed copy to a bank branch.
  3. Scan the bill QR code where the app supports it.
  4. Match the payable amount with the screen value.
  5. Keep the receipt or take a screenshot.
  6. Confirm the posting with a status check in a day or two.

Cash counters and post offices accept the printed duplicate too. Nothing about it looks unofficial. Pay with confidence.

One habit avoids most disputes. Also keep the receipt until the next bill shows your payment.

Payment safety

Pay only through safe channels

The duplicate copy itself costs nothing. No official site asks a fee for it. Free means free.

Money sent through forwarded links often vanishes. Open your own wallet or bank app instead. A known branch works too.

IESCO never asks for card numbers, OTP codes or a confirmation call after payment. A caller who demands them is not from the company. So hang up instead.

Our payment guide compares every channel in one place.

Due dates

Avoid late fees with one small habit

A late fee rarely arrives as a surprise. The warning sits on the copy you already hold.

Download when the bill lands

Check the portal on the exact day your sub-division usually receives bills. The copy takes a minute. Do it before the monthly rush.

An early copy keeps the due date visible. Nothing relies on memory afterwards.

Note the due date first

The first screen shows the due date beside the amount. Screenshot it at once or write it down.

Most late fees start the day after the due date. One glance now saves the surcharge later.

Respect app cut-off timing

Bank and wallet payments can lag at night or on Sundays. Aim to pay before the final evening.

The posting date decides the surcharge question. A receipt alone may not protect you. So time it right.

Watch the next bill

A late payment surcharge appears as a line item on the next bill. It sits beside the taxes and other charges.

Spot it next month and fix the habit. Small amounts repeat every cycle otherwise. Do not repeat it.

Final-day routine

Paying on the last day? Do it in the morning. Then keep the receipt screenshot until the status shows paid.

Use the bill status guide afterwards to confirm the posting.

Expert tips

Expert tips from daily use

These habits come from daily bill handling. Each one takes seconds to set up.

One folder per year

Save each PDF into a folder named by year. Months stack inside in order. The history stays readable at a glance.

This also settles payment arguments with landlords and banks.

Reference number in your notes

Keep the number in a locked notes app or a contact entry. Label it with the exact wording from the bill.

Typing from notes also beats squinting at a faded photo every month.

Learn your sub-division code

One part of the reference number points to your sub-division. An eye for your code block catches a wrong number fast.

The same code shows up on notices and receipts.

Use the history table

Every IESCO bill prints a small history table. Past months, with units and amounts. A sudden jump tells you to check the meter or an appliance early.

Act before the complaint window closes on a real error.

Print two copies for bank work

Banks sometimes keep one copy with the deposit slip. Carry two and skip a return trip.

Nothing extra needs signing or stamping. The printed bill speaks for itself.

Verify the name before proof use

Read the consumer name on the copy carefully. A mismatch can stall loans and transfers at the final step.

Sort out a name error at the sub-division office first. It saves trouble later.

Avoid mistakes

Common mistakes to skip

Typing the U or R letter

Some IESCO bills print a U or R letter beside the number. That letter marks Urban or Rural. The lookup wants digits only.

Leave the letter out of the box. The field counts digits, not letters. Digits only.

Trying a CNIC, name or meter number

Public IESCO lookup skips all three. The meter number describes the device alone.

But exactly two keys work here. The 14-digit reference number and the 10-digit customer ID.

Dropping a 10-digit ID in the reference field

The two fields run separate checks. A customer ID entered as a reference returns a blank result.

Switch the field label first, then paste the number.

Paying a stale copy

A duplicate pulled from two months back still shows an old amount. Paying it can leave the current month unpaid.

Delete old PDFs after settlement or move them into the history folder.

Printing straight from the phone screen

A cropped or stretched print often gets refused at bank counters. Save the PDF first and print the full page.

Keep the barcode strip visible at the bottom.

Ignoring the surcharge line

The current copy shows any late payment surcharge inside the amount. Skim past it and the next bill repeats the cost.

So read the amount line fully before you confirm anything.

Safety

Safety warnings

Pay only through official IESCO, bank or wallet channels.

A duplicate bill never needs an agent, a fee or a confirmation call.

Check the domain before typing anywhere.

Reject any caller who asks for an OTP or card details.

In doubt, call 118.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

How can I get my IESCO duplicate bill online?

Open the Get Duplicate Bill page. Enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 10-digit customer ID also works. The current bill opens on screen. You can then download the PDF or print it.

Is an IESCO duplicate bill free?

Yes. The official copy costs nothing at all. No login and no account are needed for the lookup. Anyone charging a fee is running a separate service.

Can I pay at a bank with a printed duplicate bill?

Yes. Banks, ATMs and post offices accept the printed copy. Save the PDF first, then print the full page with the barcode strip. Keep a spare copy for your records.

Can I check my IESCO bill by CNIC, name or meter number?

No. Public IESCO lookup skips all three. Use the 14-digit reference number from an old bill instead. The 10-digit customer ID is the second option.

Why is my new IESCO bill not showing online?

Bills post in batches after meter reading. Your sub-division may sit in a later batch. Check again the next day. A longer delay deserves a call to 118.

How do I save the IESCO bill as a PDF on my phone?

Open the bill page and tap the browser menu. Pick Print, then choose Save as PDF. The share sheet hides the same option on a phone. The file lands in your downloads.
