Check the dates before calling it a wrong reading

A wrong meter reading on an electricity bill can be worrying, especially when the amount jumps. Before filing a complaint, compare the dates, meter number, readings, and printed snapshot. A photo you take today may show a higher reading than the one recorded a few days earlier.

If the bill still appears inconsistent, keep the evidence and ask your distribution company to check its reading record. NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual gives a seven-day handling target for billing complaints about wrong readings. That is not a promise that every revised bill will be issued within seven days.

Quick answer

Quick answer

If your electricity bill has a wrong meter reading, start with these five checks:

  1. Match the meter number on the bill to the meter at your property.
  2. Find the bill's reading date, present reading, previous reading, and total units.
  3. Compare the bill's printed meter snapshot with the meter display.
  4. Take a clear, unedited photo of the meter and note when you took it.
  5. Submit a written complaint through your DISCO's official service centre, sub-division office, or one-window counter.

Ask for a complaint number and response date. Keep copies of the bill, meter photo, and complaint acknowledgement.

Guide section

What to check on the electricity bill

Look at the billing details before deciding what is wrong. The bill usually provides several fields that help you compare the record with your meter.

Bill detail What to check
Meter number Does it match the meter installed at your property?
Previous reading Compare it with the prior bill, if available.
Present reading Compare it with the meter snapshot and consider the reading date.
Reading date Note when the company recorded the reading.
Total units Check whether the difference between readings broadly matches the billed units and any factor printed on the bill.
Meter snapshot Check the displayed digits and any printed date or time details.

NEPRA's revised Consumer Service Manual says meter-reading snapshots are mandatory and are printed on bills. It also says DISCOs must retain these records for twelve months for use in billing disputes. The Manual calls for time-stamping and geo-tagging the snapshots. It says consumers should receive an SMS with total units and the scheduled and actual reading dates.

The snapshot can help explain what the meter reader recorded. If the printed image is blurry or missing, ask the company to check the retained reading record.

Guide section

Compare the reading with the meter safely

First, confirm the meter number. Some properties have more than one meter, and comparing the wrong display can lead to a mistaken complaint.

Next, note the reading date printed on the bill. A meter reading taken today is not automatically comparable with a reading the company recorded earlier. The display normally continues to change as electricity is used.

Take a clear photo of the meter display. Include the meter number where possible. Note the date and time, and do not edit the image. Keep the original file. Do not remove a seal, open the meter, or touch its wiring.

For a standard meter, subtract the previous reading from the present reading as a first check. For example, if the previous reading was 15,290 and the present reading was 15,482, the difference is 192 units before considering any factor or special meter setup shown on the bill. If the bill's unit figure differs, ask the company to explain how it calculated it.

Do not assume a reading is wrong just because the bill is higher than last month. Usage can change, billing periods can differ, and charges can include more than energy units. If the reading arithmetic itself looks inconsistent, include that calculation in your complaint.

Guide section

Wrong reading or faulty meter?

These are related but different problems.

The recorded reading appears wrong

The bill may show the wrong meter number, an incorrect digit, or a snapshot that does not match the recorded reading. This is a billing-record issue. Ask the DISCO to compare the bill with the reading snapshot and correct the bill if the evidence confirms an error.

You suspect the meter is inaccurate

Sometimes the display matches the recorded reading, but you suspect the meter is running fast, running slow, or malfunctioning. That is a different question: is the meter itself measuring accurately?

The CSM describes a separate meter-challenge process. It lists a checking fee of Rs 500 for a single-phase connection and Rs 1,000 for a three-phase connection, or another fee approved by NEPRA. After payment, the DISCO is to check the meter in the consumer's or representative's presence within seven working days. Confirm the current fee and procedure with your DISCO before paying. Do not use the meter-challenge route just to request a correction to an obvious reading-entry error.

How to complain about a wrong meter reading

Use an official channel and make the request specific. Our electricity bill complaint guide explains how to prepare a documented case. NEPRA's CSM says billing complaints can be registered at the Sub-Divisional Office, Consumer Service Center, or One-Window Operation of the DISCO. The Manual also says the consumer should receive an acknowledgement with a definite reply date.

  1. Gather the documents. Keep the disputed bill, the previous bill, your meter number, and a clear meter photo with its date and time.
  2. Write down the mismatch. State the reading printed on the bill, the date shown, the meter display reading, and when you took your photo. Explain the difference in simple terms.
  3. Submit the complaint. Use the DISCO's official service centre, relevant sub-division office, or one-window channel. Use the contact information printed on your bill or published by the company.
  4. Request a record check. Ask the company to review the reading snapshot and verify the present and previous readings. Request a corrected or reissued bill if it confirms an error.
  5. Keep the acknowledgement. Save the complaint number, submission date, response date, and every reply. Follow up through the same case.

Sample complaint message

Subject: Request to verify meter reading on bill

Please review the meter reading shown on my bill for [bill month]. The bill shows meter number [meter number], previous reading [reading], present reading [reading], and reading date [date]. My clear meter photo was taken on [date and time]. The figures appear inconsistent because [brief explanation]. Please check the reading snapshot and billing record, correct the bill if an error is confirmed, and provide a complaint number and response date.

Send account details and photographs only through the DISCO's official channel. Do not post your reference number, CNIC, address, or full bill publicly.

Guide section

What does the seven-day complaint timeframe mean?

NEPRA's CSM lists billing errors caused by wrong meter readings or wrong charge calculations for redressal or reply within seven days after the complaint is received. The Manual also says these complaints are monitored by DISCO complaint cells.

Treat this as a complaint-handling timeframe. It does not guarantee that every case will receive a final corrected bill, refund, or other outcome within seven days. A complicated case may require the DISCO to explain what it checked or request more information.

Keep the complaint acknowledgement because it records when the company received your case. If you do not receive a response by the date given, follow up with the same case number and ask for the status in writing.

Guide section

If the company does not resolve the complaint

First follow up through the DISCO's complaint channel. Ask for the result of its reading-snapshot review and a written explanation if it considers the bill correct.

NEPRA's complaint guidance advises consumers to approach their distribution company first. If the issue is not redressed, review the current NEPRA Consumer Affairs complaint portal requirements. Keep the original complaint, acknowledgement, bills, and relevant meter photos ready. Follow the portal's current instructions and submit only information it requests.

Do not assume that sending a complaint to NEPRA automatically cancels a bill or prevents late charges. Ask the DISCO in writing what to do about the bill while its review is pending.

Guide section

What about the due date and late charges?

If the due date is close, contact the DISCO promptly and explain that you have challenged the reading. Ask whether it can review the bill before the due date and whether any payment or due-date instructions apply while the case is open. Keep its response with your complaint record.

Do not assume that a complaint automatically extends the due date, waives a late-payment surcharge, or pauses other bill requirements. The applicable instructions can depend on the case. Get guidance from the DISCO through an official channel rather than relying on an informal promise.

Guide section

Common mistakes to avoid

Comparing readings from different dates

A current photo may show more units because it was taken after the company's reading date. Record both dates before comparing the numbers.

Assuming every increase proves a meter error

Usage, billing days, and other bill lines can change the total. Check the readings and units separately from the rupee amount.

Mixing up a reading correction and a meter test

A wrong digit or snapshot mismatch calls for a review of the recorded reading. A suspected inaccurate meter may require a separate accuracy check and fee.

Sending an unclear or cropped photo

Keep the meter number and all reading digits visible. Save the original image with its timestamp.

Making the complaint only by phone

A phone call can be useful, but keep a written case number or acknowledgement. It gives you a record for follow-up.

Waiting until after the due date

Raise the issue promptly and ask the DISCO what to do while it checks the bill. Do not assume the complaint pauses payment requirements.

Frequently asked questions

How can I prove a meter reader recorded the wrong reading?

Keep the bill, its reading date and printed snapshot, and a clear photo of the meter. Ask the DISCO to compare the retained reading record with the bill. A photo taken later should be dated because the meter continues to register use.

Why is today's meter display higher than the bill reading?

The bill may use a reading taken days or weeks earlier. Compare the date on the bill with the date and time of your photo before deciding that the recorded reading is wrong.

How long does a wrong-reading complaint take?

NEPRA's Consumer Service Manual lists redressal or reply within seven days after the DISCO receives a complaint about a wrong meter reading or calculation. This is not a guarantee of a final corrected bill within seven days.

Can I ask for the meter photo used on my bill?

The CSM says meter-reading snapshots are mandatory, are printed on bills, and must be retained for twelve months. If the snapshot on your bill is unclear or missing, ask your DISCO to check its retained record.

Is a meter test the same as correcting a wrong reading?

No. A reading-entry or snapshot mismatch is a billing-record issue. A meter accuracy challenge is a separate process and may require a fee. Confirm the current fee and instructions with your DISCO.

Will my complaint automatically extend the due date?

Do not assume so. Ask the DISCO for written instructions about payment and any due-date relief while it reviews the case.
