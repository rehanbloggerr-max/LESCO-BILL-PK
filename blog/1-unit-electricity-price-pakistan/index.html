What the 1 unit electricity price in Pakistan really is

Search for the 1 unit electricity price in Pakistan and every site gives you a different number. Here is why that happens.

A unit is one kilowatt-hour. Run a 1,000-watt appliance for one hour and you have used one unit. That is all the word means.

Your meter counts these units. Nothing is guessed. So the number on the bill is measured. The bill subtracts last month's reading from this month's reading. The difference is what you pay for.

Here is the part that confuses everyone. One unit has no single price in Pakistan. Not one. The price depends on two things. Your consumer category. And the total units you used that month.

For example, a lifeline home pays Rs 3.95 for its first 50 units. A large unprotected home pays Rs 47.20 for every unit past 700. Same grid. Same electricity. A twelve-fold difference in price.

That is why the 1 unit electricity price in Pakistan looks different everywhere you look. They are quoting different slabs. Most of them are not lying. Most of them just forget to say which slab they mean.

The good news is that the number is not random. It follows a published table. Anyone can read it. Once you know your category and your slab, the price of your next unit is predictable to the paisa.

But where people get hurt is the gap between the slab rate and the amount that actually leaves their pocket. That gap has names on your bill. Several of them. We will get to each one.

This page gives you the whole table first. Then it shows what those slabs cost you in practice. Once the fixed charge, the adjustments and the taxes land on top, the number changes a great deal.

Quick answer

The short answer: Rs 3.95 to Rs 47.20

One unit (1 kWh) of electricity in Pakistan costs between Rs 3.95 and Rs 47.20 in base tariff. That range comes from SRO 279(I)/2026, which took effect on 12 February 2026. Lifeline users pay the least. Protected homes pay Rs 10.54 to Rs 13.01. Unprotected homes pay Rs 22.44 to Rs 47.20. Your real cost per unit runs Rs 13 to Rs 65 once taxes land.

That is the honest range. Here it is, split by category.

Your categoryWhat one unit costs you
LifelineRs 3.95 to Rs 7.74
ProtectedRs 10.54 to Rs 13.01
UnprotectedRs 22.44 to Rs 47.20
CommercialRs 37.44 to Rs 39.76

Which one are you? It is decided by history, not by this month alone. Our protected consumer guide walks through the check. Stay under 200 units for six straight months on a connection under 5 kW and you are protected. Cross 200 units once and the account drops to unprotected rates for six months.

Lifeline is narrower still. It applies to the smallest connections. Lifeline consumers get no slab benefit at all. Their first 50 units cost Rs 3.95 and the next 50 cost Rs 7.74, with nothing in between.

Most homes in Pakistan fall into the unprotected band. That means Rs 22.44 at the very bottom. And Rs 47.20 at the top.

But that is still not the number on your bill. The gap between the slab rate and what you actually pay causes the most confusion. In fact, it is the most misunderstood thing about a Pakistani electricity bill. So that is the next section.

Two rules that catch people out

Rule one. Only protected consumers get the one-previous-slab benefit. A protected home using 150 units pays Rs 10.54 on its first 100 units. The remaining 50 units cost Rs 13.01.

Rule two. Unprotected consumers get no such benefit. They pay the landing slab. At 300 units, all 300 units are billed at Rs 33.10. The first 100 do not stay cheaper.

That difference is why an unprotected bill jumps so hard when consumption rises.

Commercial connections

Up to 5 kW costs Rs 37.44 per unit. Add Rs 1,000 per consumer each month. Above 5 kW costs Rs 39.76 per unit, plus Rs 1,250 per kW each month.

Time-of-use, 5 kW and above

Peak units cost Rs 46.85 and off-peak units cost Rs 34.53, with a fixed charge of Rs 675 per kW. Homes below 5 kW can ask their company for a time-of-use meter.

How to find your category on the bill

Look at the top of the bill, near your name and address. The tariff or consumer category line says Protected, Unprotected or Lifeline. A connection under 5 kW also shows A-1, which is the residential tariff code.

If the word Protected is missing, you are unprotected. In other words, the default is the costlier band. There is no third option to look for.

What sanctioned load means

Sanctioned load is the capacity your connection was approved for, measured in kilowatts. It is printed on the bill, usually near the meter number. A small flat is often 1 kW to 3 kW. A house running two air conditioners is usually 5 kW or more.

The fixed charge multiplies that kW figure by the rate for your slab. A 3 kW connection in the 201 to 300 band pays 3 × Rs 350 = Rs 1,050. It pays the same whether it used 201 units or 299. The charge does not vary inside the band.

That is worth checking carefully. A sanctioned load recorded higher than the one you applied for inflates every bill you will ever receive.

Here is a real calculation. A household uses 300 units in a month. It is unprotected. Its sanctioned load is 2 kW.

This is why bills shock people who only ever read the tariff table.

What pushes the number up

The fixed charge. This is billed per kW of sanctioned load. The rate rises with your slab. A 5 kW connection in the top band pays Rs 3,375 before using a single unit.

FPA. The fuel price adjustment. NEPRA revises it every month. So the Rs 2.50 used in the example above is only an illustration. Your own bill may carry a different figure.

QTA. The quarterly tariff adjustment. The cycle running now covers September to November 2026 at Rs 0.5194 per unit.

GST at 18%. Applied to the subtotal, surcharges included. This is why every extra line costs you more than its face value.

Then there is electricity duty. It is a small provincial levy. The rate varies by province. Our calculator uses 1.5%. Last comes the flat Rs 35 PTV licence fee.

The same exercise for a protected home

A household uses 150 units. Its sanctioned load is 1 kW. The first 100 units bill at Rs 10.54 and the remaining 50 at Rs 13.01. Energy comes to Rs 1,704.50. The fixed charge is 1 kW × Rs 300 = Rs 300. FPA, QTA, duty, GST and the TV fee bring the total to Rs 2,965.

That works out to Rs 19.77 per unit against a slab average of Rs 11.36. The gap is narrower than the unprotected one. Protected rates are low enough that the fixed lines do far less damage.

Why GST makes every extra line worse

GST is charged at 18% on the subtotal, not on the energy charge alone. The fixed charge, the FPA and the QTA all sit inside that subtotal.

So a Rs 100 fuel adjustment does not cost you Rs 100. It costs you Rs 118. Every surcharge arrives with its own 18% shadow attached to it.

This is the arithmetic that makes Pakistani bills feel larger than the tariff table suggests. The slab rate is real. It is simply not the whole story.

If your bill jumped and none of this explains it, read further. Our guide to why an electricity bill suddenly gets high walks through the nine usual causes.

Do it yourself

Work out your own per-unit cost in 10 seconds

You do not need an app. You need your bill and a calculator. That is all.

1. Find the payable amount within the due date. Not the total after the due date.

2. Find the units consumed for the month.

3. Divide the first number by the second.

4. Compare it with the slab table above.

5. If the gap is wider than about Rs 18, read your fixed charge and FPA lines.

Worked through: payable Rs 13,823, units 300. Divide and you get Rs 46.08 per unit.

One warning. A figure above Rs 60 a unit is a red flag. On a normal domestic connection, that usually means one thing. Your company is billing the fixed charge on a higher sanctioned load than you think. Check the kW printed on your bill against the meter outside your house.

Three mistakes make this calculation come out wrong. Here they are.

Using the total after the due date. That figure includes the late payment surcharge, which has nothing to do with your unit rate.

Dividing by the wrong units. Use the units consumed for the month, not the meter reading. They are different lines on the bill.

Including arrears. If you are clearing an old balance, your per-unit figure will look far worse than it is.

If the number still looks wrong after all three, the fixed charge is the next place to look. Compare the kW printed on the bill with the meter outside your house.

You can also estimate your bill from your units before it arrives.

The cliff

The same 200 units, protected and unprotected

This month's bill does not decide your protected status. Your history decides it. Stay under 200 units for six straight months on a connection under 5 kW and you qualify.

200 units, protected201 units, unprotected
Energy chargeRs 2,355.00Rs 6,653.10
Slab usedRs 10.54 then Rs 13.01Rs 33.10 on every unit
Fixed charge per kWRs 300Rs 350
Total bill, 1 kW, all taxes inRs 3,922Rs 9,133
Paid per unitRs 19.61Rs 45.44

At 200 units a protected home pays Rs 19.61 per unit. At 201 units the account turns unprotected and every unit re-rates to Rs 33.10. The same household pays Rs 45.44 per unit.

One unit. Roughly Rs 5,200 more across the month.

The good news is that the status comes back. Six clean months under 200 units restores it. The clock restarts from the month you drop back under the line, not from the month you apply.

Also, nothing needs to be filed. No form needs to be submitted. The company tracks the pattern and the bill header changes on its own.

One practical warning. The six months run consecutively. A single heavy month in the middle resets the count. A summer heatwave can undo five clean months in one billing cycle.

The change is silent. No letter arrives. Nothing warns you. You find it on the bill header, where the words Protected or Unprotected are printed.

Karachi

K-Electric and the ten DISCOs: one tariff

Pakistan uses a uniform domestic tariff. The base price of one unit is the same in Karachi as it is in Lahore, Islamabad or Peshawar. City makes no difference.

The same everywhereDiffers by company
The slab ratesTiming of the monthly fuel adjustment
The fixed charge bandsProvincial electricity duty
GST at 18%Company-specific surcharges
The Rs 35 PTV licence feeMeter rent

So a 300-unit home pays the same base rate whichever company supplies it. The final total moves by a small amount depending on where you live.

K-Electric is a privately run utility, which surprises people who expect different rates. It still bills the national residential slabs. The February 2026 revision applies to it in exactly the same way.

Karachi residents do notice two differences. First, the monthly fuel adjustment, which KE publishes on its own schedule. Second, Sindh's provincial electricity duty. Neither changes the base price of a unit.

Wrong 1 unit electricity price figures still online

Search for the price of one unit and you will find numbers that stopped being true months ago. A dozen sites still publish a 1 unit electricity price in Pakistan that February 2026 superseded. Some were never true at all.

You can check any rate claim yourself in about two minutes. Open your latest bill. Find the tariff line and the rate printed there. Compare it with the table on this page for the slab you landed in.

They should match exactly. If they do not, one of three things is happening. Your bill predates 12 February 2026. You are reading the wrong slab. Or the website you were on is out of date.

However, be careful with pages that quote a single "average" rate for the whole country. An average across lifeline, protected, unprotected and industrial consumers describes nobody's actual bill. It is a number that sounds authoritative and means nothing for your household.

Two sources are worth opening yourself. NEPRA publishes the SRO 279(I)/2026 notification as a PDF. IESCO also maintains a published tariff guide that reproduces the same tables in a far easier format.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

What is the price of 1 unit of electricity in Pakistan in 2026?

The base price of one unit ranges from Rs 3.95 to Rs 47.20. That range is set by SRO 279(I)/2026, effective 12 February 2026. Lifeline consumers pay Rs 3.95 to Rs 7.74. Protected consumers pay Rs 10.54 to Rs 13.01. Unprotected consumers pay Rs 22.44 to Rs 47.20 depending on the slab their total monthly units fall into.

How many rupees is 1 unit of electricity? (1 unit kitne ka hai)

One unit kitne ka hai is the most searched form of this question. The base answer is Rs 3.95 to Rs 47.20. What you actually pay is higher, because the fixed charge, FPA, QTA, GST and duty sit on top. For a typical unprotected home the real figure is Rs 46 to Rs 65 per unit. Divide your bill total by your units to get your own number.

Is 1 unit the same price for all electricity companies in Pakistan?

Yes, for the base rate. Pakistan uses a uniform domestic tariff. All ten ex-WAPDA DISCOs and K-Electric apply the same slab rates. What differs is the timing of the monthly fuel adjustment. Also different are the provincial electricity duty, meter rent and company-specific surcharges.

Why is my per-unit cost higher than the slab rate?

Because the slab rate is only the energy charge. Your bill also carries a fixed charge per kW of sanctioned load. Then come the monthly fuel price adjustment and the quarterly tariff adjustment. On top of those sit 18% GST on the subtotal. There is also a provincial electricity duty of about 1.5%. A flat Rs 35 PTV fee applies too. At 300 units these push the cost from a Rs 33.10 slab rate to Rs 46.08 actually paid.

How do I work out my own per-unit cost?

Take the payable amount within the due date and divide it by the units consumed for the month. A bill of Rs 13,823 over 300 units works out to Rs 46.08 per unit. If your figure is more than about Rs 18 above your slab rate, look harder. Check the fixed charge and FPA lines on the bill.

What is 1 unit of electricity equal to?

One unit equals one kilowatt-hour, written as kWh. It is the energy used when a 1,000-watt appliance runs for one hour. Your meter measures these units. The bill charges you for the difference between this month's reading and last month's.

When do these electricity rates change next?

The current schedule was notified on 12 February 2026 and the next rebasing falls around 15 January 2027. Two lines move more often than that. The fuel price adjustment is revised every month. The quarterly tariff adjustment changes every quarter. The cycle running now covers September to November 2026.
