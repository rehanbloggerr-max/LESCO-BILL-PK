Your LESCO bill is lost, late or simply not in your hands. The payment deadline does not wait for the postman. The good news: the billing record already exists online. You can open it yourself.

A LESCO duplicate bill is an official re-print of your current bill. Same month, same amount and the same due date. It works for payment at banks, wallets and post offices across the country.

This guide covers the full duplicate-bill task in plain steps. It shows the exact field for your number. It also covers the download and print route, plus fixes for common portal problems.

In a hurry? Start with our LESCO checker. It sends your number straight to the official page and opens the current bill in a new tab.

Quick answer

How to get your LESCO duplicate bill online

You can get your LESCO duplicate bill free on the official PITC page. Enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 7-digit customer ID also works. Then download it as a PDF or print the copy. Banks and wallets accept this copy for payment.

Also, the online copy carries the same consumer name, address, amount and due date as the printed bill. Pay from it directly. It is a full billing document, not a rough estimate.

No login is needed. No fee applies. The lookup works on a phone browser at any hour.

Read the six steps below. They show the exact route with checks at every stage.

The basics

What a LESCO duplicate bill is and when it helps

The amount and due date stay the same

A duplicate shows the current month's payable amount and its due date. Nothing extra is added for using the online copy.

One warning. The amount can change after the due date passes. A late payment surcharge appears on the next bill.

Banks, wallets and post offices accept it

A printed duplicate plus your normal payment goes through the same bank counters and post offices. Nothing special is required. Clerks scan or type the reference number. The payment posts to the same account.

Also, wallet apps need only the reference number. You can pay from the app without printing anything at all.

Five moments when people need one

The bill got lost in the post. It never arrived at all. Someone is handing over a house or shop. A bank or employer asked for proof of your bill. Some people simply file them.

Also, for a handover, download the current copy on the spot. Match the meter reading date and the reference number before you hand anything over.

When you still need the sub-division office

A few tasks need a stamped or certified copy from the office. Policy and legal paperwork sit in this group. The online PDF alone may not pass there.

Visit your sub-division office with the CNIC and the reference number. Ask for the document you actually need. Staff will tell you the fee and the timing.

Step by step

Check your LESCO duplicate bill in six steps

The whole lookup needs a phone, a quiet minute and one number. Most readers finish in under a minute. The official PITC bill page for LESCO loads the bill straight from the billing system.

  1. Open the LESCO bill page from our checker.
  2. Read the 14-digit reference number from any old bill.
  3. Type the number in the matching field and press submit.
  4. Check the month, payable amount and due date on screen.
  5. Download the PDF or print the copy.
  6. Pay through your bank, wallet or a bank app.

Enter digits only. Then skip spaces and hyphens. Some bills print a trailing U or R letter. Do not type it. Watch the field label before you type anything.

A wrong digit returns a blank result. Then retype slowly and compare every block against the photo. That beats five rushed tries.

Use the reference number field for the long number. Use the customer ID field only for the 7-digit ID. Not sure about your number type? See reference number vs customer ID.

Number recovery

Where to find your reference number again

The number is not gone. It is printed somewhere you already own.

Check the top of an old bill

The reference number sits near the top of every LESCO bill, close to the consumer name and address. It is the longest number in the block.

Our reference number location guide labels the exact box for you.

Read the format carefully

The number runs 14 digits and splits into sections. Each section carries part of your batch, sub-division and consumer number. An area letter appears on many bills.

Copy all 14 digits in one go. Do not cherry-pick from memory. Photo first, then type.

Try the customer ID instead

The 7-digit customer ID sits near the same block. Most LESCO lookups accept it as a full alternative.

This route saves time on a cracked or faded bill. Fewer digits means fewer typos too.

Mine your old receipts

Wallet apps and bank apps keep an electricity payment history for months. Open the history and look for the LESCO entry.

The receipt often stores the reference number or the consumer ID. Screenshot it before it scrolls away.

Ask the last payer

In a rented house or shop, the previous tenant usually paid the bill. Ask them for a photo of any recent copy.

The property owner or a family member can also send a quick picture. Verify the address before using the number.

Troubleshooting

LESCO duplicate bill not showing? Fix these first

A blank result is a lookup problem first. Official support comes later, after the easy checks.

The new month is not uploaded yet

LESCO posts bills in batches after meter reading rounds finish. Your area may sit in a later batch. The page can still show last month for a day or two.

After that, recheck the next day and look at the issue date on screen. Do not pay an old month twice.

A valid number returns an error

Check the number against the photo one more time. Watch for a missing digit in the middle sections. A short number fails quietly.

Also confirm the field itself. Reference numbers and customer IDs do not mix.

The page is stuck or slow

The official page gets busy around due dates. A frozen form often clears after a short wait. Try again on a stable connection.

Refresh once, then wait. Repeated taps can submit the same request many times.

The screen shows an older bill

This usually means the new bill has not reached the portal yet. The batch pattern explains it. Nothing is wrong with your connection.

Compare the bill month with today's date. An old copy is not this month's bill.

Someone told you the number changed

Ignore forwarded messages about a new reference format. The number printed on your latest bill always works. Older bills keep working too.

The reliable source is the bill itself or the 118 helpline. Random social posts are not.

Call the 118 helpline

The LESCO helpline answers billing questions on 118. Keep the reference number and CNIC nearby before you call. Note the complaint number after the call.

Office contact details also live on the official LESCO website. For wider portal failures, use our bill not found guide.

Print and save

Print or save the copy for later

The bill opens as a normal web page. Your browser turns it into a file or paper.

  • Use the browser menu and pick Print.
  • Choose Save as PDF on Android or iPhone.
  • Check the month and amount on the preview screen.
  • Name the file with the month, like lesco-2026-10.
  • Keep one copy in cloud storage or email.
  • Print a second copy for bank visits.

On a phone, the share sheet hides the PDF option behind one tap. Open the page menu first. The option usually appears as Save as PDF or Print in the same list.

Check three values before you save. The month, the payable amount and the due date.

Banks may want the printed copy, not a screenshot. A clean full-page PDF survives printing better than a photo.

Reference number in your notes

Verify the name before proof use

Avoid mistakes

Common mistakes to skip

Trying the CNIC or meter number

Public LESCO lookup does not search by CNIC. The meter number identifies the device, not the account.

But the system only understands two keys. The reference number and the customer ID.

Believing the 15 digit advice

Some old pages call the reference number 15 digits. The correct count is 14. Count before you type.

A made-up digit does not unlock anything. It only wastes attempts.

Paying through random links

Links shared in groups can point to fake payment pages. The official route never arrives as a forwarded link.

Type the app or site yourself. It takes ten seconds.

Downloading before upload

Checking before the month's batch posts shows yesterday's bill. The copy then misleads the payment.

So watch the month field on the first screen. That single check prevents the whole problem.

Paying a stale copy

A duplicate from two months ago still shows an old amount. Paying it may leave the current month unpaid.

Delete old PDFs after payment or move them to the history folder.

Ignoring the surcharge stamp

The current copy marks any late payment surcharge inside the amount. Skim past it and you pay late again.

Read the amount line fully before confirming anything.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How can I get my LESCO duplicate bill online?

Open the official LESCO bill page and enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 7-digit customer ID also works. The current bill opens on screen. You can then download the PDF or print it.

Is a LESCO duplicate bill free?

Yes. The official copy costs nothing. No login and no account are needed for the check itself. Anyone charging a fee for a duplicate is not part of the official service.

Can I pay at a bank with a printed duplicate bill?

Yes. Banks and post offices accept the printed copy for payment. A clerk reads the reference number and the amount. Keep a second copy for your records.

Can I check my LESCO bill by CNIC or meter number?

No. Public LESCO lookup skips both CNIC and meter number. Use the 14-digit reference number from an old bill instead. The 7-digit customer ID works too.

Why is my new bill not showing on the portal?

Bills post in batches after meter reading. Your area may be in a later batch. Then recheck the next day. A longer delay deserves a call to the 118 helpline.

How do I save the bill as a PDF on my phone?

Open the bill page and tap the browser menu. Pick Print and then choose Save as PDF. On a phone, the same option sits in the share sheet. The file lands in your downloads.
