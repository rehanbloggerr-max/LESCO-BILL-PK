A MEPCO duplicate bill is the official re-print of your current month's bill. The name, address, amount and due date stay exact. It carries full validity at every payment counter. Nothing about the consumer name, the address, the payable amount or the due date changes on the copy.

This guide walks through the whole task one plain step at a time. The number, the page, the download and the payment. The MEPCO fixes each get a full section further down.

Quick answer

How to get your MEPCO duplicate bill online

You can get your MEPCO duplicate bill free from the official PITC page. Enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 10-digit customer ID works too. Download the PDF or print the copy for the bank. Free and official.

The official MEPCO website points the same way. Its menu label is MEPCO Bill and it leads into the PITC bill system. The official MEPCO SMART app pulls the same copy.

No account is needed. No fee applies. The lookup works on any phone at any hour.

Read the six steps below for the full route. Each step ends with a small check.

Basics

What counts as a MEPCO duplicate bill

The name sounds formal. The document is fully official. Here is what the copy contains and what it is for.

It is an official re-print, not a new bill

The duplicate comes from the same billing record behind your paper bill. So nothing gets edited on the way. The figures arrive exactly as the system holds them.

That makes the copy as valid as the original for every normal use. Keep it as your working copy and pay from it.

The amount and due date stay the same

A duplicate shows the current month's payable amount and its due date. Nothing extra is added for using the online copy.

But the amount can change once the due date passes. A late payment surcharge then appears on the next bill. That fee is avoidable.

Banks, wallets and post offices accept it

A printed duplicate goes through the same bank counters as any normal paper bill. Banks, ATMs and post offices all accept it. A clerk reads the reference number and the amount. The clerk at any bank counter reads the same reference number printed on your original paper bill.

Wallet apps only need the reference number. Pay from the app without printing anything at all. Utility stores take it too.

Four common moments for a copy

The bill never reached the house. It arrived torn or badly faded by monsoon rain. A buyer wants the full property papers before the transfer. A bank asked for proof of address.

For a property handover, pull the current copy on the spot. Match the meter reading date and the reference number first.

When the sub-division office still matters

A few tasks need a stamped or certified copy. Legal paperwork and some policy matters fall in this group. The plain PDF may not pass there.

Then visit your sub-division office with the CNIC and the reference number. Staff will tell you the fee and the timing.

Step by step

Find the number

Where to find your reference number again

The number hides in plain sight on every bill. One of these routes will bring it back.

Look at the top-left Ref No box

The reference number sits in the top-left box of every MEPCO bill. It appears just above the barcode strip.

Our reference number location guide labels the exact box for you.

Read the print carefully

The number runs 14 digits on a MEPCO bill. The label reads Ref No right above the barcode.

Copy all 14 digits in one go. Start with a clear photo, then type. Careful beats fast here.

Try the customer ID instead

The 10-digit customer ID sits in the same consumer block. Most MEPCO lookups accept it as a full alternative.

This route saves time on a faded or torn bill. A shorter number also means fewer typing slips.

Mine your old receipts

Wallet apps and bank apps store an electricity payment history going back months. The receipt usually shows the reference number or the customer ID.

So screenshot the receipt before it scrolls away. That copy stays readable forever.

Ask the last payer

In a rented house or shop, the previous tenant usually paid the bill. A photo of any recent copy solves the problem.

The owner or a family member can send a quick picture too. Verify the address before you use the number.

Fix it

MEPCO duplicate bill not showing? Fix these first

A blank result has a short list of causes. Work through them in this order. No panic needed.

The new month is not uploaded yet

MEPCO posts bills after meter reading rounds, usually two to three days later. Your circle may sit in a later posting batch. The page can still show the previous month for a day or two.

Then recheck the next day and read the issue date on screen. Never pay an old month twice. One month, one payment.

A valid number returns an error

Compare the number against the photo one more time. Then watch the middle digits most. Slips cluster there.

Also confirm the field. The reference box rejects a 10-digit customer ID.

The page is stuck or slow

The official page gets busy around due dates. A frozen form often clears after a short wait. A stable connection helps too.

Refresh once, then wait a minute. Repeated taps can send the same request many times.

An old number after a meter change

A new meter or a shifted connection can bring a fresh reference number. The old digits may stop opening the current bill.

Use the number printed on the latest bill. For a stubborn failure on the old one, call 118.

An estimated reading month

A locked meter room leads to an estimated reading on some rural bills. The bill still posts with a K mark. The amount can look odd against your own meter.

Note the reading and the mark on the copy. Then compare both against the meter dial.

Call the 118 helpline

MEPCO answers billing questions on 118. Keep the reference number and CNIC nearby before the call. Note the complaint number afterwards.

The MEPCO website lists 118 and the toll-free 0800-63726 line. Written complaints go through the official CCMS portal. For wider portal failures, our bill not found guide covers more cases step by step.

Print and save

Print or save the copy for later

The bill opens like any web page. Your browser then turns it into a file or a sheet of paper.

  • Use the browser menu and choose Print.
  • Pick Save as PDF on Android or iPhone.
  • Check the month and amount on the preview first.
  • Name the file with the bill month.
  • Keep a cloud copy beside the downloads folder.
  • Then print one spare sheet for the bank.

Now the bank rule. Save the PDF first, then print the full page at 100 percent zoom. The barcode strip at the bottom must stay whole and unscaled. Stretched phone prints get refused at busy counters. A full-page PDF prints identically on any printer and keeps the barcode strip whole for the counter.

Due dates

Avoid late fees with one small habit

A late fee rarely arrives as a surprise. The warning sits on the copy you already hold. Read it once.

Download the day the bill lands

The portal usually shows the bill two to three days after the meter reading. Pull the copy before the monthly rush.

An early copy keeps the due date visible. Nothing relies on memory afterwards.

Note the due date first

The first screen shows the due date beside the amount. Now screenshot it at once or write it down.

Most late fees start the day after the due date. One glance now saves the surcharge later.

Respect bank cut-off timing

Bank and wallet payments can lag at night or on Sundays. Aim to pay before the final evening.

The posting date decides the surcharge question. A receipt alone may not protect you. So time it right.

Request an extension in the app

Genuine crunches have an official lever. The MEPCO SMART app carries a due date extension request. Approval rests with the office.

Use it before the due date, never after. The surcharge line knows no mercy otherwise.

Watch the next bill

A late payment surcharge appears as a line item on the next bill. It sits beside the taxes and other charges.

Spot it next month and fix the habit. Small amounts repeat every cycle otherwise. Use the bill status guide afterwards to confirm the posting.

Expert tips

Expert tips from daily use

These habits come from daily bill handling across the cotton belt. Each one takes seconds to set up.

One folder per year

Save each PDF into a folder named by year. Months stack inside in order. The history stays readable at a glance.

This also settles payment arguments with landlords and banks.

Reference number in your notes

Keep the number in a locked notes app or a contact entry. Label it with the exact wording from the bill.

Now typing from notes also beats squinting at a faded photo every month.

Learn your circle code

One part of the reference number points to your sub-division. An eye for your code block catches a wrong number fast.

The same code shows up on notices and receipts.

Use the 12-month history

Every MEPCO bill prints a small history of past months with units and amounts. A sudden jump tells you to check the meter early. A quiet month next to a loud one tells you more than any calculator about your home.

Act before the complaint window closes on a real error.

Print two copies for bank work

Banks sometimes keep one copy with the deposit slip. Carry two and skip a return trip.

Nothing extra needs signing or stamping. The printed bill speaks for itself.

Verify the name before proof use

Read the consumer name on the copy carefully. A mismatch can stall loans and transfers at the final step.

Sort out a name error at the sub-division office first. It saves trouble later.

Avoid mistakes

Common mistakes to skip

Adding spaces or dashes

The field wants all 14 digits in one unbroken run. Spaces and dashes fail the check.

Type from a clear photo or your notes. Then re-read the middle digits once.

Trying a CNIC, name or meter number

Public MEPCO lookup skips all three. The meter number describes the device alone.

But exactly two keys work here. The 14-digit reference number and the 10-digit customer ID.

Dropping a 10-digit ID in the reference field

The two fields run separate checks. A customer ID entered as a reference returns a blank result.

Switch the field label first, then paste the number.

Paying a stale copy

A duplicate pulled from two months back still shows an old amount. Paying it can leave the current month unpaid.

Delete old PDFs after settlement or move them into the history folder.

Printing from a phone screenshot

A cropped or stretched print often gets refused at bank counters. Save the PDF first and print the full page.

Keep the barcode strip visible at the bottom.

Ignoring the surcharge line

The current copy shows any late payment surcharge inside the amount. Skim past it and the next bill repeats the cost.

So read the amount line fully before you confirm anything.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

How can I get my MEPCO duplicate bill online?

Open the official MEPCO bill page and enter the 14-digit reference number from any old bill. The 10-digit customer ID also works. The current bill opens on screen. You can then download the PDF or print it.

Is a MEPCO duplicate bill free?

Yes. The official copy costs nothing at all. No login and no account are needed for the lookup. Anyone charging a fee is running a separate service.

Can I pay at a bank with a printed MEPCO duplicate bill?

Yes. Banks, ATMs and post offices accept the printed copy. Save the PDF first, then print the full page with the barcode strip. Keep a spare copy for your records.

Can I check my MEPCO bill by CNIC, name or meter number?

No. Public MEPCO lookup skips all three. Use the 14-digit reference number from an old bill instead. The 10-digit customer ID is the second option.

Why is my new MEPCO bill not showing online?

Bills post a few days after the meter reading round. Your circle may sit in a later batch. Check again the next day. A longer delay deserves a call to 118.

I lost my reference number and every old bill. What can I do?

Check bank and wallet receipts first. The reference number sits on most payment slips. A sub-division office can also reprint the number with your CNIC and address.
