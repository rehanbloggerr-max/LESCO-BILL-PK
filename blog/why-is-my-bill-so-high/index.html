You opened this month's bill. The total made you blink twice. Nothing changed. Yet the number jumped anyway.

Take a breath. You want to know why your electricity bill is so high. The answer is usually printed on the paper. You just have to know where to look.

This guide lists the nine usual suspects. Each one comes with a two-minute check you can run at home. Grab this month's bill and last month's copy. The online bill view shows a full twelve-month history. Nothing gets lost.

One rule before we start: compare units first, rates second. One column shows your units. The next one shows the price per unit. Read both together and the mystery almost always breaks open.

One note on the numbers here. They match the current NEPRA schedule our calculator uses. Company and category cause small differences. The paper in your hand is the final word.

Quick answer

The short answer

Three causes explain most high electricity bills in Pakistan.

More units. Summer cooling pushes consumption up fast. One AC running at night can add 150 units a month.

A slab jump. Crossing 200 units can move your whole account to a costlier category. Two extra units can cost thousands.

The tax stack. GST, duty and fixed fees sit on top of the energy charge. On big bills they can add a third to the total.

The nine causes below cover everything else. Each section tells you where to look on the bill.

You do not need any app for this. You do not need to visit an office. You need the bill, last month's bill and about ten minutes.

The causes

The 9 causes of a high electricity bill

1. Your units actually went up

Summer is the usual suspect. An AC running eight hours a night adds roughly 150 to 250 units (kWh) a month. Fridges work harder in heat too. Water pumps run longer.

Check in 2 minutes: open the Units Consumed column and compare it with last month. A rise means the rate did nothing wrong. That is good news. The fixes live in the next-bill section below.

One honest note. Sometimes usage rises and you did nothing different. A heatwave does it. A fridge with a worn seal does it. Guests staying over does it. Higher units are not always a fault. They are still the cheapest problem on this list to fix.

2. You crossed a slab

Pakistan's tariff works in steps. More units means a higher per-unit rate. Lifeline units cost Rs 3.95 to Rs 7.74. The protected slabs run from Rs 10.54 to Rs 13.01 per unit. The unprotected slabs run from Rs 22.44 up to Rs 47.20. And the moment you cross 200 units you lose something else. The one-previous-slab benefit that protected accounts get.

Crossing a slab line hurts badly. The protected or unprotected test decides which ladder you are on. At 199 units the bill stays on protected rates. Cross to 201 and the account turns unprotected. Every unit then bills at the rate of the band your total lands in. At 201 units, all 201 bill at Rs 33.10. Not the first 200 at a cheaper rate. Two extra units. Thousands of rupees more.

Check in 2 minutes: look at the units figure. Is it just above 100, 200, 300 or another slab line? Our bill calculator shows the exact slab math for your number.

3. You lost protected status

Protected status belongs to households that stay under 200 units. You keep it with six clean months in a row. One heavy month breaks the streak.

After that, the account drops to unprotected rates. The bill prints your status near the tariff code. The fall is silent. No letter arrives.

Check in 2 minutes: find the words Protected or Unprotected on the bill header. A flip right there is your answer. Six clean months win it back.

The math is brutal. Here it is. A protected household at 100 units pays Rs 1,054 for energy, plus Rs 200 per kW of sanctioned load in fixed charges. An unprotected household at the same 100 units pays Rs 2,244 for energy, plus Rs 275 per kW. Same consumption. More than double the money. This single line is the most expensive switch on any Pakistani bill.

4. The fuel line moved (FPA/FCA)

The Fuel Price Adjustment reconciles fuel costs every month. It prints as a per-unit line on the bill. It can be positive or negative.

On a 400-unit bill, a Rs 2 FPA line adds Rs 800. People miss this line constantly. A two-rupee figure looks harmless on the page. It multiplies across every unit.

Check in 2 minutes: find the FPA or FCA line. Multiply it by your units and compare with last month's figure.

5. A quarterly adjustment landed (QTA)

NEPRA reconciles bigger costs every quarter. The result lands on bills as the QTA line. Sometimes it charges. Sometimes it gives relief.

Real 2026 example, straight from NEPRA determinations: bills from March to May carried +Rs 0.3504 per unit. Bills from June to August carried a relief of Rs 1.9857 per unit. Same house, same usage, opposite effect.

Check in 2 minutes: look for a QTA or quarterly adjustment line. The NEPRA tariff explainer covers how these numbers get made.

6. Your bill was estimated

Sometimes the reader does not come. The system then issues an average or estimated bill. It guesses from your history. Guesses can overshoot.

The bill itself tells you. Look for the word Estimated near the reading fields. A flat, repeated units figure across months is another hint.

Check in 2 minutes: read the meter yourself. Compare that number against the reading printed on the bill. Wide gap? The estimate was wrong. The next bill quietly settles the difference. Usually in both directions.

Estimated bills annoy people for one very fair reason. The correction waits. It lands in one lump later. One scary bill arrives. The next one looks strangely cheap instead. Same money. Keep dated photos of your meter every single month. They settle these arguments fast.

7. The tax stack grew with the bill

Taxes scale with your usage. A bigger energy charge pulls a bigger GST along with it. The current stack on domestic bills:

LineWhat it is
GST18% on the taxable amount
Electricity dutyA percentage-based government levy
TV licence feeFixed Rs 35 per month
SurchargesVary by consumer category

Here is real math from our calculator. Take 550 unprotected units on a 3 kW connection. Energy alone: 550 × Rs 40.22 = Rs 22,121. The fixed charge adds Rs 675 per kW of sanctioned load: 3 × Rs 675 = Rs 2,025. The current quarterly adjustment (QTA) adds Rs 0.5194 per unit: Rs 286. Electricity duty adds 1.5% of the energy charge. Then 18% GST lands on the whole subtotal. Add the Rs 35 TV fee. Before any monthly fuel adjustment (FPA) you are near Rs 29,300. With the FPA line our calculator applies by default, the same 550 units come to about Rs 30,878. The energy number you remembered was never the bill.

Check in 2 minutes: add up every line under the tax block. Compare it with the energy charge. On big summer bills the tax block often reaches a third of the total. That is normal, not a scam. Annoying. Still normal.

8. A late surcharge slipped in

Miss the due date and the next bill carries a surcharge. It prints as its own line. One forgotten week can add hundreds of rupees.

Check in 2 minutes: look for Late Payment Surcharge on the bill. Then check last month's due date against your payment receipt. The status guide covers paid-but-unpaid delays.

9. The reading itself is wrong

Meters can over-register. Connections can leak. A neighbour's line might touch yours after repairs. Rare. Still real.

A detection bill is the other version of this story. Sometimes the company suspects theft or a meter fault. Then comes a heavy assessment bill. Those need an office visit with papers, not a guess.

Check in 2 minutes: note today's meter reading. Wait one full normal day. Everything switched off and the meter still spinning? Call 118 the same day.

Taxes

The uncomfortable truth about taxes

Pakistani bills carry heavy taxes. This surprises people every summer.

On a small protected bill the taxes hide easily. On a 500-unit summer bill they scream. GST dominates. The 18% rate makes it the biggest line after energy. Duty and surcharges ride along. The Rs 35 TV fee stays small.

None of this money stays with the power company. The federal board receives the GST. Provincial coffers take the duty. The DISCO plays postman with all of it. Fair or not? Argue it over dinner. The math itself is not in debate.

Complaints

When to complain, and how

Complain only after the checks above point to a real error. A wrong reading. A status that flipped without notice. A bill that ignores your payment record. Evidence moves complaints. Anger moves nothing.

  1. Call 118 first and note the complaint number. It is the company helpline. Keep your reference number ready.
  2. File on the CCMS portal for a written trail.
  3. Visit the sub-division office for detection bills and meter faults. Carry the bill, an old bill and your CNIC.
  4. Keep every stamped receipt. Papers win arguments.

Give the office seven working days before escalating. That is the norm, not a brush-off.

Two more habits help. Write down the name of whoever takes your complaint. Take a photo of every document you hand over. Offices lose paper. Your phone does not.

Next month

Six ways to shrink next month's bill

  1. Protect the status. Near the 200 line? Stay under it for six straight months. Protected status is the cheapest rate in the country.
  2. Move heavy loads off peak. Run the washing machine and iron in the morning. Evening hours cost more on time-of-use tariffs.
  3. Set the AC at 26°C. Each degree lower adds roughly 5% to cooling cost.
  4. Check the FPA line monthly. Know the number before the bill tells you.
  5. Pay before the due date. The surcharge is the dumbest tax on the paper.
  6. Run your units through the calculator mid-month. No more end-of-month shocks.

None of this requires sacrifice. It requires reading the bill once a month instead of dreading it. The people with calm summers are not richer. They just know their number by the 15th. Weighing solar? Read the net metering changes before you sign anything. The export rate moved sharply in February 2026.

Checklist

The one-minute checklist

  • Units compared with last month
  • Slab line checked (100 / 200 / 300)
  • Protected status read off the header
  • FPA line multiplied by units
  • QTA line spotted and dated
  • Tax block totalled
  • Reading checked if nothing matched
FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Why is my bill high when the units are the same?

Look at the rate side of the bill first. The FPA moved. A QTA landed. Your protected status may have flipped in between. One of these three lines holds the answer.

How do I know if I am a protected consumer?

The bill header prints Protected or Unprotected near the tariff code. Protected means under 200 units for six straight months.

Where is the FPA line on my bill?

Below the energy charge, named FPA or Fuel Price Adjustment. It shows a per-unit amount. Multiply by your units for the real cost.

What if my bill was estimated?

Read your meter and compare. The next bill adjusts the difference. Huge gap? File a complaint with both readings.

My meter seems faulty. What now?

Switch everything off and watch the meter. Still moving? Call 118 the same day. Ask for a meter test.

Are the taxes on my bill legal?

Yes. GST at 18%, electricity duty and the Rs 35 TV fee are government levies. The DISCO collects them, not keeps them.

How long does a complaint take?

118 registers it instantly. Resolution normally takes about seven working days. CCMS gives you a tracking number for follow-up.
