Start here

Your neighbour used 180 units last month. So did you. Their bill came to roughly Rs 2,900 and yours to roughly Rs 5,900. Nothing about the wiring explains that. The reason is a single word printed in the consumer block of your bill: protected, or unprotected.

Most people never look at that line. Then one summer month pushes them over 200 units. The bill stays high for half a year, even after usage drops back. This guide shows you where the line sits. It explains what the rule actually says. And it covers the three wrong versions that circulate online.

More

The short answer

Yes, you can get it back. The official CCMS portal at ccms.pitc.com.pk verifies you by registered mobile number or by CNIC. It then lists your connections with the reference number beside each one. That single route solves the problem for most people in about five minutes, and it costs nothing.

Seven more routes sit behind it. Some are faster if you happen to have a particular scrap of paper. Some are the only option left when the account was registered decades ago under a relative's name. This guide covers all eight, in the order worth trying.

One clarification up front, because it confuses almost everyone. You cannot check a bill using a CNIC. The PITC bill portal accepts a reference number or a customer ID and nothing else. But you can use a CNIC to recover the reference number. Same card, two completely different questions. Most guides online give one answer to both and call it a day.

Triage

Start here: what do you still have?

The bill is gone. The number is not on your phone, not in a drawer, not in a message. Before you try anything, spend one minute deciding which situation is yours. It saves a wasted afternoon.

Ask yourself four questions. Do you have any earlier bill as a photo or a PDF? Do you know the 10-digit customer ID? Is the mobile number on the account still active in your name? And do you know which company supplies you? Your answers pick the route.

Two things are worth knowing before you start. The number never changes month to month, so recovering it once solves the problem permanently. And none of these routes cost anything. Kisi ko paise dene ki zaroorat nahi.

Customer ID

Way 1: the 10-digit customer ID

Why the short number gets overlooked

Most people hunt for the long reference number and forget the shorter one sitting next to it. The customer ID, also printed as consumer ID, is 10 digits. Several PITC company portals accept it as a valid alternative.

Open your company's portal and look for the option selector above the input box. Choose customer ID rather than reference number, then type the digits without spaces. Press search. If the portal supports it, the current bill loads exactly as it would from the longer number.

This is not universal. A few companies treat the customer ID as an enquiry field and return nothing on the bill page. If the page stays blank after two clean attempts, stop repeating yourself and move to CCMS. Baar baar try karne se kuch nahi hoga.

Not sure which of the two numbers you are holding? The reference number against customer ID guide shows both. It lists their digit counts and where each one sits on a printed bill.

CCMS portal

Way 2: the CCMS portal that opens without a number

This is the route almost every guide misses. The Customer Complaint Management System sits at ccms.pitc.com.pk. It is the official PITC portal, run for the Ministry of Energy. It is built for filing complaints, but its verification step does something useful. It accepts three different identifiers, and two of them are not the reference number.

Three ways to verify yourself on CCMS
Verify byWhat you typeWhat comes back
Reference numberThe 14 digits from an old billOne connection, straight away
Registered mobileThe number the account was registered withConnections linked to that mobile
CNICYour 13-digit identity card numberEvery connection under that CNIC

The two options that matter here

The mobile and CNIC options are the ones you want. Select the search type from the dropdown, enter the digits, and the portal lists the connections it finds. Click the one you need and the reference number appears with the account details.

One wrinkle catches people out. A single CNIC often holds more than one connection. A home and a small shop, or an old meter never formally closed. The list will show all of them, so match on the address or the sub-division printed beside each row. Ghalat connection choose kar lena aam ghalti hai.

The catch is that CCMS matches the mobile or CNIC recorded when the connection was set up. Ported your number? The search may fail. The same happens when the account was registered decades ago under a relative's details. In that case the helpline or the office route is the one that works.

Helpline

Way 3: call 118 and ask

The PITC contact centre runs on 118 from any phone in Pakistan. It is the same number for power breakdowns and billing questions, and it is free on most networks. Ask for your reference number and be ready to verify yourself.

What to have ready before you dial

Have four things ready before you dial. Your CNIC number. The full address on the connection. The registered name. And the meter number, if you can read it. The agent searches their internal database and reads the number back to you.

Every company also runs its own line. These tend to be less busy than 118 and the staff know the local records better.

Mobile apps

Way 4: the company mobile app

Several companies have official apps that store your connection after a one-time setup. MEPCO SMART and LESCO Light are the two most established. Both are built by PITC or the company itself, so the data comes straight from the billing system.

The setup is the same shape in each. Download the app. Register with your name, CNIC, mobile and email. Then tap the plus icon and add a meter. Some versions ask for the 10-digit consumer ID at this step. If you do not have it, register anyway and check whether the app lists existing connections against your CNIC.

Once a meter is added, the home screen shows the current bill. It also shows the paid or pending status and several months of usage history. From that point the number is stored permanently on your phone. Ek baar set up kar lo to phir kabhi nahi dhoondna parta.

Receipts

Way 5: dig through your payment history

Almost everyone has paid a bill at some point through a wallet or a bank. Those receipts carry the reference number, and most people never look at them again. This route works offline and takes about three minutes.

Start with your message inbox

Search your messages for the company short name first. Terms like LESCO, MEPCO, IESCO or WAPDA usually surface an old confirmation. If you paid in cash at a branch, the counterfoil may still be in a drawer.

In person

Way 6: go to the sub-division office

This is the slowest route and the most certain. Every company has sub-division offices, and the staff there can search by name, address and CNIC. Use it when the digital routes have failed or when the account was registered long ago under someone else's details.

Ask at the revenue or consumer desk rather than the complaints counter. Say plainly that you need the reference number for online bill checking. Most offices write it on a slip of paper or read it out. Photograph the slip before you leave the building.

K-Electric

K-Electric uses a different system entirely

None of the above applies if you are in Karachi. K-Electric runs its own billing platform, not the PITC portal. So CCMS will not find your connection. The 118 helpline does not carry KE records either.

What you need is the 13-digit account number printed on any KE bill. It is shorter than a DISCO reference number and formatted differently. That is why copying it into a PITC checker returns nothing. Our K-Electric bill guide shows exactly where it sits on the statement.

To recover it, call KE customer service on 118 or 021-99000. Or visit a KE customer centre with your CNIC. KE also has an app and a WhatsApp service. Both look up the account against a registered mobile number.

The 8334 myth

The 8334 shortcut does not do what guides claim

This one deserves its own section because it wastes so much time. Plenty of guides list the SMS service as a way to recover a lost reference number. Type PITC, a space, and the number, they say, and send it to 8334.

Read the instruction carefully

Look at what that instruction actually requires. You have to type the 14-digit number to get a reply. If you had the number, you would not be reading this page. The service sends back the bill amount and due date for a reference number you already hold. It is a bill reminder, not a lookup tool.

The other shortcode, 8118, is for registering complaints. It is genuinely useful, but it also expects a reference number or a registered mobile. Neither shortcode can go hunting for you. Jo cheez kaam nahi karti, usse baar baar try karke waqt zaya na karein.

Check the bill

Got the number? Check the bill now

Once you have it, the lookup itself takes seconds. Select your company, type the digits with no spaces, and open the duplicate. Our guide to checking any bill online walks through the five steps with screenshots of each field.

Three quick checks stop the usual errors. Make sure the company you picked matches the one supplying you. A MEPCO number entered under LESCO returns nothing. Count the digits before you press search. And use the newest bill, not a screenshot from years ago, because reference numbers are occasionally revised.

If it still comes back blank, the bill not found guide covers the six reasons a correct number can fail. For the paid or unpaid question, the status check guide explains what each status means.

Escalation

When none of it works

Sometimes every route comes back empty. The usual causes are simple. An account registered under a previous owner's CNIC. A connection transferred years ago without a name change. Or a mobile number that no longer exists. These are record problems, not lookup problems.

The escalation path is short. File a complaint on CCMS describing the mismatch and keep the ticket number. If nothing moves in a reasonable window, escalate to PMDU, the Power Division's monitoring unit, quoting that ticket. Beyond that sits NEPRA, which oversees the companies.

While that runs, you can still read the current month from the meter yourself. The missing bill guide explains how to estimate what you owe. That way a late payment surcharge does not build up during the delay.

Habits

Four habits so you never search again

  • Photograph every bill the day it arrives. One folder on your phone beats any filing system.
  • Save the number in a contact. Create a contact called "Bijli reference" and paste the digits in the notes field.
  • Register your mobile on the connection. It unlocks the CCMS route and the company app.
  • Pay one bill through a wallet. The receipt keeps the number permanently in your transaction history.

Do two of these and the problem disappears. Jo kaam ek minute mein ho sakta hai, uske liye dobara sub-division office jana zaroori nahi.

Safety

Three things a genuine service will never do

A lost reference number is a common worry, and that attracts people who charge for free information. The rules are simple once you know them.

No official portal asks for payment to look up a number. Neither CCMS nor the company portals charge anything, and the 118 helpline is free. If a page asks for a fee, close it.

Be careful where you type your CNIC. Use ccms.pitc.com.pk or an official company domain, and check the address bar before entering anything. A CNIC plus an address is enough for real damage. An unknown site does not deserve it.

And no agent can change your tariff category, reduce a recorded reading, or delete arrears for a fee. Those are record corrections that only the company can make. Anyone promising otherwise is selling you nothing.

FAQs

Frequently asked questions

Can I check my electricity bill with a CNIC number?

Not directly. The PITC bill portal accepts a 14-digit reference number or a 10-digit customer ID only. Your CNIC still works as a recovery key. Enter it on the official CCMS portal and it lists the connections registered against you, each with its reference number.

How do I find my reference number if I have no old bill?

Start with the official CCMS portal. It can verify you by registered mobile number or by CNIC. Once the search returns your connection, the reference number is shown on screen. If that fails, call 118 with your CNIC and address.

Does the 10-digit customer ID open a bill?

Usually yes. Most PITC company portals accept it as an alternative to the 14-digit reference number. Select the customer ID option rather than the reference number option before you type. A few companies treat it as enquiry only, so switch to CCMS if nothing loads.

Can I get my reference number by SMS to 8334?

No. That shortcode sends you the bill amount and due date for a number you already have. It cannot look anything up. This is the most repeated mistake in the guides online. Use CCMS or the 118 helpline instead.

What happens when I enter my CNIC on CCMS?

The portal lists every connection registered against that CNIC. One identity card often holds two or three meters, especially where a family has a home and a shop. Pick the connection you want and the reference number appears with it.

Is the route different for K-Electric?

Yes. Karachi runs on K-Electric's own system, not the PITC portal. You need the 13-digit account number printed on a KE bill. CCMS and the 118 helpline do not cover KE connections.

How long does a sub-division office take?

Usually under an hour if you carry your CNIC and the address matches their record. Rush hours are the first week after bills are issued. Take a copy of your CNIC and any old bill fragment you can find.

Should I pay anyone who offers to find my reference number?

No. Every route in this guide is free. The official portals charge nothing and so does the helpline. A person asking for a fee is selling you something you can get yourself in two minutes.
