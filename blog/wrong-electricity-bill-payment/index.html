Safe next steps

What to do after paying the wrong reference

  1. Save your payment record

    Keep the original receipt, confirmation message or transaction screen. Take a screenshot of visible payment details.

    Write down:

    • The transaction ID or reference.
    • The date, time and amount.
    • The reference number entered during payment.
    • The reference number on the bill you meant to pay.
    • The payment method and provider.
    • Any complaint or support ticket numbers.

    Never share your password, PIN or one-time code. Use only official support channels.

  2. Contact the payment provider

    Contact the bank, wallet or payment service used for the transaction. Use its official app, website, phone number or branch.

    Tell the provider the transaction completed. The electricity reference number may be wrong. Ask the provider to trace the transaction and explain its process. Request a complaint or ticket number. Keep a written response.

    For a pending or failed transaction, ask the provider to confirm its status first. A debit notification alone does not prove payment reached your intended bill.

  3. Contact the electricity company

    Contact the distribution company linked to the receipt’s reference. A distribution company is often called a DISCO. Contact your intended bill’s company too.

    Share the transaction ID, amount, date and both reference numbers through an official channel. Ask the company to check its records and explain its process.

    Official sources do not confirm a universal right to recover or transfer this payment. Ask the company how it handles the case. Get its answer in writing. Keep the complaint number.

  4. Keep both sides updated

    Your provider can trace its transaction. The electricity company can check its billing records. Either side may request your receipt or more payment details.

    Keep a record of each contact. Note the date, channel, complaint number and response. A provider may send you to another support team. Ask for the instruction in writing.

Official complaint route

How to complain through PITC CCMS

For complaints handled through PITC’s Customer Complaint Management System, its website lists these channels.

The Register Complaint page lets you search by a 14-digit reference or 12-digit mobile number. Its form shows fields for Complaint Category, Complaint Type and Complaint Details. Use the details field to explain the two references and include the payment transaction ID.

  • Call 118.
  • Send an SMS to 8118.
  • Email ccms@pitc.com.pk.
  • Submit the complaint through the PITC CCMS website.

CCMS advises written complaints with supporting documents. Its website says complaints are acknowledged within 24 hours. It sets a 14-day resolution target. Complex cases may take longer. CCMS says it will notify consumers about an extension.

Those are CCMS’s stated targets, not a refund or transfer promise.

Check the bill

What if the payment succeeded but your bill still shows unpaid?

Check the reference number on the payment receipt first.

Receipt shows your intended reference: Contact the payment provider and electricity company. Ask them to trace the transaction and confirm its status.

Receipt shows another reference: Treat it as a wrong-reference payment. Contact the provider and company linked to the paid reference. Share both reference numbers. Ask what process applies.

Payment is pending: Ask the provider to confirm its final status before paying again.

Payment decision

Should you pay the intended bill again?

There is no blanket answer. The first payment may not have reached the intended account. The electricity company may still show an amount due.

Check the bill’s due date. Contact the provider and electricity company today. Ask the provider for an update on your complaint. Ask for written advice about the unpaid bill and late-payment risk.

Don’t assume the wrong-reference payment cleared your intended bill. A second payment won’t guarantee the first one comes back.

Complaint records

What to include in your complaint

You can adapt this message:

I made an electricity bill payment on [date and time] for Rs [amount]. The transaction ID is [ID]. The receipt shows reference number [paid reference], but I intended to pay reference number [intended reference]. Please trace the transaction and tell me the official process for this case. Please share my complaint number and reply in writing.

Attach the receipt and a copy of the intended bill. Send personal documents only through an official secure channel. Submit them only after a request.

Escalation

If your bank does not resolve the complaint

For bank complaints, the State Bank of Pakistan’s consumer guidance says customers should complain to their bank first. It describes escalation to the Banking Mohtasib Pakistan for eligible cases.

A customer may approach the Banking Mohtasib after 45 days without a bank response. A customer may also approach it after an unsatisfactory response. It does not cover microfinance banks. SBP lists a separate route for microfinance customers.

The applicable route depends on the institution and complaint. Check official instructions before escalating. For utility-side follow-up, use the electricity company’s official complaint process. Check the current NEPRA Consumer Service Manual for applicable service rules.

Quick answers

Common questions

Can I get my money back after paying the wrong electricity reference?

It depends on the provider’s and electricity company’s investigation. The official sources reviewed here do not confirm a universal refund guarantee.

Can the payment be moved to the correct bill?

Ask the relevant electricity company and payment provider. Do not assume a transfer is available or automatic.

Who should I contact first?

Contact the payment provider responsible for the transaction. Also contact the company for your intended bill.

What if the payment is successful but the wrong bill remains unpaid?

Check your receipt first. A different number means you paid another reference. Report the payment to the provider and electricity company. Keep complaint numbers and written replies.

What proof should I keep?

Keep the receipt, transaction ID, amount, date and time, both reference numbers, intended bill and complaint records.

Should I share my bank PIN or one-time code with support?

No. A legitimate support process should not require your password, PIN or one-time code.

Final check

Bottom line

Check the receipt before deciding what happened. Contact the payment provider and relevant electricity company. Keep a written record. Track the intended bill’s due date. Ask for written instructions about any unpaid amount.
